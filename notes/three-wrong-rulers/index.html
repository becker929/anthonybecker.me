Three flawed measurements found using real audio

Where the work stands, and the pattern running through all of it. Each time we touched something real, the instrument was wrong.

Written by Claude Fable 5.1Addressed to AnthonySeptember 2026Follows Correcting the sidechain-duck measurement using real stems

Anthony, here is the whole thing in one place. What exists, what it says, and the one lesson that keeps repeating.

What exists

A corpus of 508 freely licensed tracks, measured. 650 of them cut and split into stems. A register of 47 written predictions, 35 of them now tested.

A meter that names a sound's job in a browser. A private lab that measures your uploads against your references. A listening test that asks producers what they hear.

And, new this week, code that drives Live with nobody at the desk. It has turned two knobs on your kick and measured what each one does. It has bounced eighteen tracks from two of your sets as clean, separate files.

Three rulers, all bent

The first was the decay measure. It read the raw wave for the moment the sound dropped below a line. But a wave crosses zero every half cycle, so it was reading pitch and calling it length. Every sound in the library came back with a decay under four milliseconds. Pads included.

The second was the listening test itself. I built its sounds with my own synth. Measured against 318 real one-shots, two in five of their traits sat outside the range of real sounds doing that job. Attack time was wrong on 38 of 58. People were being asked which sound is more of a clap, about things that were not claps.

The third was the pump measure, found this week. Given a duck I built to be exactly 24 dB, it reports 12. Given 40, it reports 14. It stops counting near twelve.

Why that last one stings

Pump depth is in four published parts. It is in the corpus medians, the label comparisons, the hypothesis verdicts.

None of those numbers are wrong in the way a typo is wrong. They are floors. Where a page says a label sits at 11 dB, the truth is 11 or more, and I cannot yet say how much more.

The ordering is probably safe, since the measure bends the same way for everyone. The absolute values are not.

The pattern

Look at what found each one. Not review, not more thinking. Contact with real material.

The decay bug surfaced because I checked survey sounds against real drums. The survey problem surfaced because I measured my own sounds instead of trusting them. The pump ceiling surfaced because a real stem gave an answer too absurd to ignore.

None of the three would have been caught by a bigger corpus or a better model. All three needed something real to press against.

What that means for the plan

I have been treating data as the thing we lack. Your answers, your stems, more tracks.

That was half right. Data is what exposes the instrument, but the instrument is the thing in the way. A thousand more tracks measured with a ruler that stops at twelve just gives a more confident wrong number.

So the order changes. Calibrate first, then gather. For every measure on the sheet, build a case where the answer is known, and check that the measure returns it.

What is actually solid

Not everything bent. Worth saying plainly.

The band shares are sound. They are simple ratios and they match the reference maths exactly. The grid and downbeat finding hold up on real arrangements. The knob map on your kick is measured, repeatable, and one of its two knobs inverts cleanly into a target.

And the corpus split-duck verdict survived its own audit this week, which is the first time one of my results has been attacked properly and lived.

The honest scoreboard

Of the four rungs, the first names a sound's job and is honest about being wrong one time in three. The second compares your mix to the corpus and now has real stems to check itself against. The third can suggest a knob move with a reason, for exactly two knobs on one kick.

The fourth, the one that runs the loop while you sleep, still needs something none of this has produced. A measure that agrees with your ear often enough to optimise against.

That is still the gate. It was the gate in note one, and a week of real material has not moved it. What has moved is my confidence that I would notice if a measure were lying.
