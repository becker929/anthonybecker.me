Beauty in the world, not the tokens

Room World can draw more than the room bot designs. Today the model writes every room from scratch. Its light, colour and noise are new each time, and often weak. This plan moves that beauty into fixed parts the model picks from. It comes in eleven small chunks, and Anthony judges each one.

Written by Claude Opus 5.5Addressed to AnthonyTuesday 3 November 2026, 02:50 UTC (Monday 18:50 Pacific)Proposed

Summary

  • Build parts once and well: light, colour, materials, noise and finishing.
  • The model spends its words on choices, not on boilerplate.
  • Two tracks run side by side. One makes the bot faster. One makes rooms better.
  • Every look change is a small round: four rooms, then your verdict.
  • A change stays only if you prefer it.
  • Total: about 60 agent-hours, two agents at most, and about $50 of test rooms.

Why change anything

Three things stand out from the bot's builds on 2 November.

First, the examples set the ceiling. The designer copies the rooms its prompt shows it. Today those are two templates and two system rooms. One of them costs three times Room World's target to draw.

Second, phones pay for clumsy code. Five of the fourteen bot rooms with phone reports draw below full resolution there. Bird Bicycles drops to 35%, at half the frame rate. Yet three of your four favourites draw at or near full resolution. Beauty does not need heavy code.

Third, the bot is slow for the wrong reason. A build takes six to nine minutes, nearly all of it waiting on the model. Drawing and checking take about 20 seconds. Builds run one at a time, so a busy hour queues up.

Principles

  • Your taste is the target. A model judge ranked the orrery rooms in reverse of you. So you judge every look change.
  • Small rounds. Two briefs, old against new, four rooms. You pick, and say why.
  • Nobody else's room changes. Room World is shared. New parts are opt-in. Bot rooms opt in by default.
  • Phones are the screen. Every part is costed on a phone before it ships.
  • Foundations before cleverness. The critique loop and the scene language come last. They build on the parts.

One change to your list

You asked for finishing that is always on. It should be on for every bot room, and opt-in for everyone else. Most rooms already finish their own colour. A second pass over them would wash them out.

One more catch. Rooms hand over finished colour, clipped to the screen's range. Real tonemapping and bloom need the light before that clip. So chunk 9 changes how rooms hand over their pixels.

Glossary

Agent-hour
An hour of one AI agent working. Rounds where it waits on you are not counted.
Bloom
A soft glow that spills around bright light.
Brief
The bot's description of the room someone asked for. The designer works from it.
Critique loop
The model looks at pictures of its own room, then fixes one thing.
Designer
The part of the bot that turns a brief into a room's code.
Finishing
Steps that run over the whole picture after the room is drawn.
Occlusion
Darkening in creases and corners, where less light reaches.
Round
Two briefs drawn the old and the new way, shown side by side.
Scene language
A short description of a room that the bot expands into code.
Shader
The code that draws a room, run for every pixel.
Tonemapping
Turning bright light into screen colour without losing contrast.
Vignette
Gentle darkening at the edges of the picture.
