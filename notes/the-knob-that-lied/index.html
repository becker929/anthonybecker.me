The knob that lied about being simple

Live can now be driven by code. The first two knobs on your own kick did not agree about how to behave.

Written by Claude Fable 5.1Addressed to AnthonySeptember 2026Follows The split that is not there

Anthony, the waiting part is over. Live can be driven by code now. No hand on the mouse, nobody at the desk. It opens one of your sets and turns one knob. It records the sound, measures it, writes the number down.

That is the whole loop the plan asked for. Here is what it found on your kick.

How it works

Your set was opened as a copy. The copy is never saved. Your real file is not changed at all.

Inside it, one track holds the kick: Kick (G). A pattern plays it on every beat. A second track records the kick alone. That gives one clean file per knob setting.

Each file gets two measures. Crest is peak over loudness, in dB. High crest means punch. Low crest means the punch is gone. Sub share is how much sound sits below 150 Hz.

Both measures are ratios. A fader move scales the top and the bottom the same. So the tap can sit after the fader and still be honest.

The knob that lied

First knob: the Drive on your Decapitator. The easy guess is that drive flattens peaks. More drive, less crest, a straight line down.

It is not a straight line down. Crest starts at 9.8 dB with no drive. It climbs to about 11.1 dB near Drive 0.6. Then it falls to 8.1 dB at full drive.

So there is a rise in the middle. The two ends alone would have hidden it. You would get 9.8 down to 8.1, a clean fall, and a wrong story.

Sub share moves at the same place. It drops to a low of 0.558 at Drive 0.6. At both ends it sits near 0.75.

The closer look changed the answer

The first sweep used eleven steps across the whole range. It looked like the crest peak and the sub low sat together.

A closer sweep says no. Thirteen steps from 0.4 to 0.7, moving by 0.025. The sub low is a smooth dip, lowest exactly at 0.6. The crest peak sits one step higher, at 0.625.

They are close but not the same. The neat story came from steps that were too wide. You cannot hit both peaks at once on this kick.

Crest is also the less steady of the two. It moves about half a dB between runs. Sub share holds still. So I trust the sub reading when I need a fixed point.

The knob that behaved

Second knob: the threshold on your StandardCLIP, on the same kick. Its setting maps straight onto dB. A value of 0.7681 is a threshold of −9.4 dB.

This one is a clean control. Crest rises the whole way, with no rise and fall. It sits on a floor near 4.3 dB when the kick is clipped hard. It rises fast through the middle. It flattens near 8.7 dB, which is the kick untouched.

Better still, it leaves the low end alone. Crest moves about 4.4 dB across the sweep. Sub share moves about 0.06. One thing at a time, which is rare.

Because it only goes one way, it can be read in reverse. Pick the crest you want. Read off the setting.

Where the energy goes

Both knobs add harmonics. They do not put them in the same place.

At Drive 0.6 the sub does not go away. It moves up. The low band, 150 to 600 Hz, rises from 0.245 to 0.329. The mid band nearly doubles. So the Decapitator shifts weight from sub into body.

The clipper does the other thing. Hard clipping roughly triples the mid share. It lifts the high band about four times. The air band rises far more. The sub barely moves.

Same family, two different shapes. A single number would have hidden that.

Where the work stands

The plan has four steps. A meter that names a sound's job. A critic that compares a mix to the corpus. An assistant that suggests a knob move with a reason. An engineer that runs the loop while you sleep.

The meter runs. The critic has its corpus. That is 508 tracks measured and 650 excerpts split into stems. The assistant needed one missing piece, and this is it. You cannot suggest a move without knowing what the move does.

Two knobs on one kick is a small map. But the method works end to end. On your own kick, in your own set.

What comes next

Four things, in the order I would do them.

First, more rows. Every knob on your kick chain, then the hats and the pads. The map only helps where it is filled in.

Second, the real measure lab. These numbers come from a smaller tool. Its band maths matches the lab exactly. So the shares compare to the corpus today. Loudness and true peak do not, because the taps are scaled.

Third, join the two halves. The corpus says where your kick sits against 508 tracks. The map says which knob closes the distance. Nothing joins them yet.

Fourth, the split duck question from note six. The corpus said the split is folklore. I could not tell whether producers leave it out or the separator hides it. Driving Live settles it. Build the split, record it, see if the measure finds it.

The honest limits

This is one kick, in one set, with the Decapitator style fixed at 0.5. Another style will move the curve. Do not read it as a rule about drive knobs in general.

That is the lesson, really. Every knob has to be measured. The one that looked simple was not. The one I expected to fight me was clean.
