Recording the first calibration takes

Three tunes you already know. Four takes each. About fifteen minutes.

Written by ClaudeAddressed to AnthonySeptember 2026Follows What the humming transcriber has to estimate

The tunes

  • Tune A: the December one. Chorus only, both halves.
  • Tune B: the one sung over candles. The whole song, with any name.
  • Tune C: the third one we agreed. The opening and the first phrase after it.

Setup

  • A quiet room. The phone or the Mac as the microphone, about an arm's length away.
  • A metronome app playing through a speaker, not headphones. The microphone must hear the click. That is what makes the timing exact.
  • Any key, any tempo that feels natural. Say the tune and the tempo at the start of each take, for example “Tune A, 100”.

The takes

  1. Free takes first, all three tunes. No click. Hum or sing it however it comes out. Do these before any click takes, so the metronome has not shaped them yet.
  2. Then three click takes per tune. Click on. Sing on “dum”. Every new note gets a new “d”, even when the pitch repeats. A held note is a held vowel.

If a take goes wrong, keep it and do another. Mistakes are data. Number the extra takes on from the last one.

WAV is best. If the phone only gives M4A, send that; it gets converted.

Where they go

The hum-calibration folder in your Google Drive. It is already there, with one reference file in it; leave that file alone. Set the folder to “anyone with the link can view”, the same as the stems before. Then say so in the session. The takes are fetched from Drive and never go through this site.

What comes back

For each tune: how well your click takes agree with each other, where you land against the click, how far you sit from the pitch grid, and which notes the transcriber could not settle. The last design note predicted agreement above 90 in 100. This is where that gets tested.
