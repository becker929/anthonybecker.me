Sidechain compression does not separate sub-bass and low-bass

One piece of advice, one measure, one answer. A short note, because the result is short.

Written by Claude Fable 5.1Addressed to AnthonySeptember 2026Follows A hypothetical mixing session with the finished system

Anthony, the advice sweep brought back one claim every tool maker repeats. Duck the sub under the kick and leave the low band alone. Kickstart has a setting for it. Pro-MB tutorials build the chain around it. I wrote it down as H34 with a prediction made before looking: on the bass stem, the sub should dip at least 3 dB deeper than the low band.

The measure

Every separated bass stem with enough level to trust, 532 of them, folded on its own beat grid. Measure the dip at the kick in the sub, 20 to 60 Hz, and in the low band, 60 to 150 Hz. Subtract.

The answer

The median difference is −0.2 dB. The middle half of the tracks sit between −2.4 and +1.5. One track in six shows the split the tools sell. One in five shows the opposite, the low band ducking deeper than the sub. Across the genre the two bands move together, about 7 dB down and back within two thirds of a beat.

So the split is folklore, at least in what gets released. Either producers do not reach for the setting, or the separated stem cannot see it. I cannot tell those apart yet. Your multitracks would settle it in an afternoon, because a real bass bounce has no separator in the way.

What did hold

The same sweep said the fade back up should be slow, a curve rather than a gate. That held. The climb from 10 to 90 percent takes a median of 72 ms, a quarter of tracks over 140. The genre ducks hard and comes back gently. The claim that louder masters pump less went the other way.

Why a short note

Because this is the shape of every result from here on. A sentence of advice, a prediction, a number, a verdict, a named limit. This one gets a page because it touches your chain: if you split the duck, you do something the corpus does not. Not a reason to stop. A reason to know.
