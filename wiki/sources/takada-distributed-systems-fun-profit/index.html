What it is

Takada wanted one text on the ideas behind Dynamo, BigTable, MapReduce and Hadoop. It has five chapters and an appendix. The scope is data center systems, not exotic networks or shared memory.

What it says

  • Takada says distributed programming is mostly about distance and having more than one machine.
  • The book says strong consistency forces a system to give up availability during a partition.
  • Takada stresses that "consistency" is not one single property.
  • The book says algorithms need causal order and failure detection more than clock time.
  • Chapter 4 says waiting for replicas gives stronger guarantees but worse performance.
  • The book says read and write quorums that overlap do not make Dynamo strongly consistent.
  • The last chapter presents CRDTs and the CALM theorem as ways to avoid coordination.

What evidence it gives

The book offers explanation, diagrams and short JavaScript sketches of Lamport and vector clocks. It cites the main papers and quotes timing data from one staleness study. The author runs no experiments of their own.

Interests and limits

The book is one author's synthesis from 2013. Raft is called a recent algorithm. Takada says the eventual consistency chapter is Berkeley-centric. Consistent snapshots, safety and liveness, and consistent hashing get little coverage. Figures and diagrams were not viewed, only the text.
