What it is

Prathamesh Sonpatki runs the SRE Stories series and published this conversation with Alex Hidalgo. Hidalgo wrote a book on service level objectives and works at Nobl9. The questions move from career to tools, trends and rest.

What it says

  • Hidalgo says a human thread runs through a career in security, support and Google.
  • Hidalgo says Google's SRE books were overly ambitious and should not be treated as holy.
  • Hidalgo says large enterprise customers varied widely, and outsiders cannot assume a company's internal culture.
  • Hidalgo says dev tool builders should remember humans use them, not just paying customers.
  • Hidalgo says many talk ideas come from long walks, which Hidalgo calls WalkOps.
  • Hidalgo says the SLO book still holds, but would cover error budgets more.
  • Hidalgo advises readers to be thoughtful about what they consume and make their own decisions.

What evidence it gives

The evidence is the speaker's recollection and opinion. The only concrete cases are Hidalgo's career steps and time on Google's customer reliability team. The interview offers no data or measurements.

Interests and limits

Hidalgo works for Nobl9, which sells tooling for service level objectives. Hidalgo also wrote a book on the topic. The conversation took place before Twitter became X, as a note says. The interview is a friendly profile, with few challenges to the claims made. Its small-talk sections on scuba diving and dogs are not summarised.
