What it is

Justin Andersun writes the newsletter Turtle's Pace. This essay argues against retreating into private effort. It sets out to persuade writers, founders and athletes to seek real opposition.

What it says

  • The post calls private grinding the Cave and says it is comfortable and feeds delusion.
  • Andersun says smart devices, algorithms and AI build a cozy Cave that flatters existing assumptions.
  • The author recounts four winters of wrestling. Better technique and conditioning did not bring wins.
  • Andersun says the real motive was avoiding shame, not seeking victory.
  • The post says any worthy pursuit needs a mat, meaning a public test.
  • It urges readers to ship, share and risk losing in public.

What evidence it gives

The main support is one extended anecdote about high school wrestling. The post also quotes the wrestling coach Dan Gable and cites an Under Armour ad. There are no studies or figures.

Interests and limits

This is a personal essay, so its lesson rests on one life. The post does not test the claim against people who gained from private practice. The author also grants that technique and conditioning mattered.
