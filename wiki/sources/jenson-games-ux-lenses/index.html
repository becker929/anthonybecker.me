How Video Games Inspire Great UX

A UX designer applies six ideas from video game design to apps.

What it is

Scott Jenson is a UX designer with over 30 years in the field. The essay records what Jenson learned from Raph Koster, author of A Theory of Fun. It aims to inspire app designers, not to copy games.

What it says

  • Jenson met game designer Raph Koster over 8 weeks to test old UX assumptions.
  • The article draws six lenses from Koster's book. Three break an app down and three build it up.
  • Story is author made. Narrative is user made, and users fill any gaps with invented stories.
  • Games are fractal. Crossing the street in Frogger nests frog movement and joystick timing, each with feedback.
  • Super Mario teaches three kinds of jump through one action. An app designer might add three buttons.
  • Jenson says Breath of the Wild taught steep-climb avoidance without Jenson noticing.
  • Games hint constantly and subtly to move players on. Apps use blocking dialog boxes.

What evidence it gives

The essay relies on examples. These include Frogger, Super Mario, Breath of the Wild and the 1984 Macintosh. The author calls the Photoshop hint example made up. There are no experiments or user data.

Interests and limits

Jenson says the work is done at Google, but the essay sells no product. The claims are one designer's reading of one book. Nothing was tested with real app users. The page text was read in full, but its screenshots and videos were not viewed.
