What it is

A long reference article with sections on epigraphy, characters, the narrative, themes and later influence. It draws on many scholars, mostly French and English.

What it says

  • The Sumerian version has 400 lines and dates to about 1700 BCE. The Akkadian version has 138 lines.
  • Inanna is stripped at seven gates and killed by Ereshkigal. Beings made by Enki revive Inanna.
  • Inanna must supply a substitute and picks Dumuzi. The damaged text allows several readings of how Dumuzi and Geshtinanna split the year.
  • Scholars disagree on whether Inanna is guilty. They also differ on what the nudity means.
  • Rites for Dumuzi are documented at Mari and Nineveh. Tammuz laments reach the Bible and later cults.
  • The article lists psychological readings from Campbell, Harding and Wolkstein, and a 2026 Kurdish film.

What evidence it gives

The article cites a large body of scholarship. It has block quotations from translations of both versions. It gives named scholars for each competing reading. It is a secondary summary, not an original analysis.

Interests and limits
