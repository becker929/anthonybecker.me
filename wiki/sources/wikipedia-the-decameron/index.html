What it is

This is the English Wikipedia entry for Boccaccio's story collection. Volunteer editors write and revise it, and no single author is named. It aims to inform a general reader about the book and its afterlife.

What it says

  • The article describes 100 tales told by ten young people sheltering from plague.
  • The article says the book was thought to be written between 1348 and 1353.
  • The subtitle Prince Galehaut points to a go-between figure from Arthurian romance.
  • Each narrator rules one day and sets its theme. Dioneo may choose any topic.
  • The article says Boccaccio borrowed the plots of almost all the stories.
  • The book was burned in 1497 and placed on the Index in 1559.
  • John Payne's 1886 version is called the first truly complete English translation.

What evidence it gives

The article gives a reference list of scholarly books and papers. It also carries a detailed table of English translations. The table's pre-1971 entries draw on a translator's introduction to the 1971 Penguin edition. Some sections are tagged as unsourced.

Interests and limits

Volunteers edit the page and it changes over time. The analysis section carries a notice about possible original research. Some claims about sources are tagged as needing citations. The article summarizes the book and does not reproduce the tales.
