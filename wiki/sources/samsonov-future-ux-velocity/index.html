What it is

Pavel Samsonov writes The Product Picnic newsletter on UX and product management. This issue continues an earlier one on the limits of the more-is-more approach to software. It tries to say what designers should do next.

What it says

  • Samsonov argues that doing more work faster will not transform the system behind a product.
  • The post says companies avoid design because doing more is safe and better is risky.
  • It quotes Simon Morgan-Wilson: service design asks why a journey has its shape.
  • Samsonov claims automation fails when real life departs from tidy use cases. Older systems resist error better.
  • The post says the industry confuses action with shipping software. This wastes tokens and trust.
  • It proposes ecosystem mapping and persona landscape mapping. The activity, not the map, creates value.

What evidence it gives

The post offers argument and block quotes. Quoted writers include Anna Tsing and Ashley Rolfmore. It links a news article and a 1993 paper. There are no data, case studies or tests.

Interests and limits

Samsonov promotes a workshop at the top. The newsletter is funded by side projects. The argument is stated, not tested. Linked essays, papers and the lecture series were not opened. One reader comment asks for worked examples.
