Why Durable Execution Should Be Lightweight

A company blog post arguing durable execution should be a library.

Subject
Distributed systems and reliability

What it is

Two DBOS authors explain durable execution, which saves program state to survive crashes. They introduce their library as a lighter alternative to orchestrator-based systems.

What it says

  • The authors say Step Functions and Temporal use an external orchestrator and workers.
  • The authors call external orchestration heavyweight, needing orchestrator and worker servers.
  • They propose a library, so workflows and steps are ordinary functions.
  • They say DBOS Transact stores workflow status, inputs and step outputs in Postgres tables.
  • They say recovery reruns pending workflows, replaying recorded step outputs.
  • They say workflows must be deterministic, with non-deterministic work placed in steps.
  • They say a library can add durable sleep, messaging and queues, all backed by Postgres.

What evidence it gives

The post offers a design description and a "Hello, world" example. It explains recovery step by step. It gives no benchmarks, user studies or comparisons run against other systems.

Interests and limits

DBOS sells products built on this library and the post promotes it. Code listings in the post were missing from the text, so only prose was read. The comparison with Step Functions and Temporal is argued, not measured.
