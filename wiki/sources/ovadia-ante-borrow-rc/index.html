What it is

Evan Ovadia, who works on the Vale language, walks through Ante's rules for mutable references. The post explains shape-stability, shared types, unions and temporary uniq conversion.

What it says

  • Ovadia says Ante blends reference counting and borrow checking for mutable objects without run-time panics.
  • Ante's shape-stability allows several mutable borrows of one struct and its fields at once.
  • Types marked shared are reference counted, and their fields can be mutably borrowed without locking.
  • A mut borrow of a union cannot yield a mut borrow of its variants.
  • A temporary uniq reference is allowed if the scope uses nothing that might alias it.
  • Ovadia says Ante's creator stressed the check needs type analysis, which could be brittle.
  • Ovadia says Rust's Cell cannot give a mutable reference to a String inside an Rc.

What evidence it gives

The post offers Ante code snippets and the compile errors the design should produce. It gives a Rust Cell comparison in an appendix. It shows no benchmarks and no tests of a running compiler.

Interests and limits

The post says Ante is a work in progress with parts still theoretical. Ovadia works on Vale, a related language, and says so in a note. The post reports no performance numbers. Ovadia, not the creator, calls the type-analysis check possibly brittle.
