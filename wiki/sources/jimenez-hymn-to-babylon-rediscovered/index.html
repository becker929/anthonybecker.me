What it is

A Phys.org page supplied by Ludwig Maximilian University of Munich. It announces a cuneiform hymn praising Babylon, now fully deciphered. It sets out what the text describes.

What it says

  • LMU scholar Enrique Jiménez rediscovered a hymn to Babylon. It had been lost for a thousand years.
  • Jiménez says an AI-supported platform found 30 more manuscripts of the hymn.
  • The hymn has 250 lines. It presumably dates from the start of the first millennium before Christ.
  • The page says the many copies suggest it was widespread. Jiménez says children copied it at school.
  • The text describes the city's buildings and how the Euphrates greens the fields.
  • The hymn gives new details on Babylonian women as priestesses. It says inhabitants respected foreigners.

What evidence it gives

The page quotes Jiménez several times and prints a short excerpt of the hymn. It cites the paper in the journal Iraq, with a DOI. The page does not show the tablets' full text or any method details.

Interests and limits

The text comes from the university that employs Jiménez, so it is promotional in tone. This reading covers the news page only, not the paper. Jiménez is quoted on "decades". The "completely decipher" claim is the press text's own. The page also carries an automatic summary written by a language model.
