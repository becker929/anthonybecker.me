What it is

Timothy B. Lee writes a plain-language explainer for the newsletter Understanding AI. It traces how reinforcement learning, after pretraining, helped make agentic models possible. It opens a week-long series on agents.

What it says

  • The author thinks AutoGPT and BabyAGI failed because small errors compounded over many steps.
  • Ross and Bagnell argued in 2011 that imitation learning suffers compounding errors.
  • In DAgger, the model drove and Ross corrected it, which worked much better.
  • Reinforcement learning trains by trial and error. A 2025 paper found it held up better out of distribution.
  • RLHF trains a text-rating model from human choices, then uses it as the reward model.
  • Constitutional AI has a judge model score outputs against written principles.
  • DeepSeek's R1 report showed reasoning length growing during training on checkable math and code problems.

What evidence it gives

The article draws on cited papers and a quoted interview with Ross. It walks through Ross's SuperTuxKart experiment. It describes a 2025 DeepMind chart, a 2022 Waymo paper and DeepSeek's R1 paper. It runs no experiments of its own.

Interests and limits

The author runs a paid newsletter, and the article promotes a subscription discount. The title promises a minimum of math and jargon. The Anthropic Opus account rests on a Semianalysis report. The author says DeepSeek's discovery was not entirely spontaneous.
