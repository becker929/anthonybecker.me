What it is

Jon Yablonski runs the site, according to its copyright line. The home page lists laws, effects and cognitive concepts. Each entry links to a longer page that was not opened.

What it says

  • The site describes itself as a collection of best practices for designers building interfaces.
  • Each named law gets a one-line definition, such as Fitts's Law, Hick's Law and Chunking.
  • The Doherty Threshold entry says productivity rises at response times under 400 milliseconds.
  • The page advertises an updated large-format poster that adds laws such as Selective Attention.

What evidence it gives

The page offers definitions only. It gives no studies, data or sources on the home page. Entries include Jakob's Law, Miller's Law, Peak-End Rule and Zeigarnik Effect.

Interests and limits

The page is a menu for deeper pages, and only the home page was read. The site also sells a poster, cards and a book. The page does not say how laws were chosen or how well each is supported.
