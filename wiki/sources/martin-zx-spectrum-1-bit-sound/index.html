What it is

Michael Martin runs Bumbershoot Software, a site of retro programming experiments. This post is part of a ZX Spectrum tour. The author says it records failures as well as successes.

What it says

  • The author planned to copy advanced PC speaker techniques but ended up with other results.
  • Playing 1-bit PCM at 16kHz leaves 219 cycles between writes, and sounds awful.
  • Arpeggiated chords, with notes switched fast, sound scratchy but clearly like a chord.
  • Summing the top bit of three counters gives a richer chord. Each loop takes 338 cycles.
  • Shuffling samples between channels beats pulse arpeggios but trails channel summing.
  • The attempt at pulse width modulation failed, giving only a high-pitched squeal.

What evidence it gives

The post gives Z80 assembly code with cycle counts for the playback and chord routines. Judgments of sound quality are the author's own listening. The code and an audio file are said to be in the author's GitHub repo.

Interests and limits

The author is the only listener, and tests ran in the Fuse emulator. The post says real hardware may sound near-inaudible without an amplifier. The author admits not knowing whether bad sounds came from bugs or from the technique. Pulse width modulation is left unresolved.
