What it is

gingerBill is the creator of Odin and writes about its design. This post is in a series on syntax. It sets out Odin's asm templates. It argues they are the best in any language.

What it says

  • gingerBill says GCC and Clang treat assembly as strings. The compiler understands almost nothing in them.
  • The post argues assembly is not untyped. Each instruction has valid operand forms, widths and clobbers.
  • Odin uses one syntax for all instruction sets, with destination first like most ISAs.
  • Odin has multiple return values. Instructions such as cpuid and div map onto asm templates directly.
  • The compiler checks each instruction against rexcode tables. Errors appear at the offending token.
  • The post says most clobbers are inferred. It says the feature took about 7 days to build.
  • gingerBill says asm templates are restricted hygienic macros, and will replace some platform-specific intrinsics.

What evidence it gives

The post gives code samples in GCC, MSVC, Turbo Pascal and Odin syntax. It describes the rexcode tables as round-tripped against llvm-mc and other tools. It reports no benchmarks, and no other language was tested.

Interests and limits

The author designed Odin and is judging the design. The claim of best in any language is stated as belief. It was not tested. The rexcode library covers many architectures, but inline assembly needs only those Odin targets.
