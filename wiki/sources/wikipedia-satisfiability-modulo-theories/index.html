What it is

Volunteer editors wrote this overview. It covers theory, solver architecture, standards and applications. The article says it was first adapted from a column in an ACM newsletter.

What it says

  • SMT asks whether a formula is satisfiable under theories such as integers, arrays and strings.
  • The article says SMT is typically NP-hard and is undecidable for many theories.
  • Early solvers encoded problems as plain SAT. Modern ones pair SAT search with theory solvers.
  • Theorem provers favour problems with many quantifiers, while SMT solvers favour large problems without them.
  • A table compares many named solvers, such as Z3 and cvc5, by features.
  • Listed uses include program verification, symbolic execution, program synthesis and program repair.
  • The SMT-LIB standard supports shared benchmarks and a yearly competition called SMT-COMP.

What evidence it gives

The article offers definitions, small example formulas and a solver comparison table. It cites papers and a handbook chapter. It gives no new measurements of its own.

Interests and limits

Volunteers wrote the article and some statements are tagged as needing a citation. The solver table notes that many SMT-LIB entries marked yes may be only partial. The article describes the field and does not rank solvers. It assumes familiarity with logic.
