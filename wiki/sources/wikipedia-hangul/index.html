What it is

The article is a reference entry on English Wikipedia. It describes the Korean alphabet from its creation under King Sejong to the present. It covers both Koreas, the diaspora and attempts to use the script for other languages.

What it says

  • The article says Hangul was officially published in 1446 through two texts under King Sejong.
  • It says the modern basic letters combine to yield 51 jamo in total.
  • It says the modern letters can form 11,172 syllables, though far fewer see use.
  • It says five basic consonant shapes are based on human speech organs.
  • It says elites looked down on Hangul for centuries, until the late 19th century.
  • It says scholars dispute whether Hangul is a featural script, with DeFrancis questioning Sampson's label.
  • It says that by 2023, 40 adults and 300 children reportedly learned Chitembo Hangul.

What evidence it gives

The article cites linguists and historians, with named scholars for each dispute. It quotes the preface of the founding text in translation. Dates and counts come from cited books, papers and government standards.

Interests and limits

Wikipedia articles are edited by volunteers and no author is named. The prose was read, but the letter tables, footnotes and reference list were not. Several claims about letter origins and script type are marked as debated. The article cites sources that were not checked.
