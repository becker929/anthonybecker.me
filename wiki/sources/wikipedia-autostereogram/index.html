What it is

Wikipedia contributors wrote this reference article. It explains how autostereograms work, how computers make them and how to view them.

What it says

  • An autostereogram is one 2D image that creates a 3D illusion without a stereoscope.
  • Repeating a pattern at a smaller spacing makes that area appear closer to the viewer.
  • A depth map is a grayscale image that sets the pixel shift for each point.
  • Bela Julesz invented random dot stereograms in 1959 while working at Bell Laboratories.
  • Christopher Tyler made the first black-and-white random-dot autostereogram in 1979, with programmer Maureen Clarke.
  • Viewing needs the eyes' focus and convergence to be decoupled, which can be trained.
  • People with amblyopia, or stereoblindness, cannot see the 3D images.

What evidence it gives

The article gives worked examples with pixel spacings of 140, 130 and 120. It cites papers by Wheatstone, Julesz and Tyler. It offers no new experiments.
