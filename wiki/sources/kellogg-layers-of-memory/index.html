What it is

Tim Kellogg's post compares three ways of working within limited context. They are Letta's layered memory, Anthropic's multi-agent research system and Cognition's single-agent advice. Kellogg says OpenAI Deep Research formatted a conversation into the post. He says he edited it lightly and that it is generally all AI.

What it says

  • Letta splits memory into core, message buffer, archival and recall blocks.
  • The post describes each block as a lossy compression of the last, like CPU caches.
  • Anthropic's system compresses in parallel, with subagents reporting to a lead agent.
  • Cognition's Walden Yan says agents share context poorly. He suggests one agent plus a model that condenses its history.
  • The post closes by treating compression as a form of cognition.

What evidence it gives

The post offers no measurements of its own. It relays Anthropic's report that a multi-agent setup did better on broad queries. That report and Yan's argument come from linked posts.

Interests and limits

Letta's archive is often a vector database, so this is not a file-based design. It appears here as a frame for deciding what a memory layer keeps. The text is largely AI-written, by the author's account.
