The power of two random choices

A short post arguing that picking the better of two random hosts balances load well.

What it is

Marc Brooker is an engineer at AWS and writes this on a personal blog. The post summarizes a survey of research by Mitzenmacher and others. It adds a toy simulation of ten servers.

What it says

  • Brooker says stale cached load data makes requests herd toward a previously quiet host.
  • The surveyed approach picks two hosts and uses the one with less load.
  • The simulation has 10 servers, one request per second, and cached queue lengths.
  • In the simulation, picking the best host does worst once the data is old.
  • Best of two led for refresh intervals from 10 to 70.
  • Brooker says best of two combines real load information with resistance to herding.

What evidence it gives

The post offers one simulation of a very simplistic system, described in prose. It points to the Mitzenmacher survey for the mathematical argument. No code or data is shown.

Interests and limits

The author works at AWS, though the post says all opinions are their own. The simulation is the author's own and uses a few simple parameters. It tests four rules against one queue model. The survey paper was not read.
