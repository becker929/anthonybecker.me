What it is

Gregory Bryant and Paul Smaldino wrote this paper for a theme issue on animal vocalizations. It offers an account of why distorted timbres arise and vary.

What it says

  • Musical distortion is noisy timbre that emulates nonlinear phenomena in animal vocal signals.
  • Listener sensitivity to these phenomena is said to have aided distortion technologies.
  • Distortion keeps evolving because groups must coordinate internally and differ from other groups.
  • The authors present an agent-based model of norm evolution for traits like distortion.
  • The model depends on a functional optimum, group cohesion and differentiation, and groupishness.
  • The authors say the account explains diversity in sounds and genres, and subgroup norms.

What evidence it gives

The abstract names a computational model as support. It mentions no listener data or recordings. Model design and results were not read.

Interests and limits

The authors have an interest in their own framework. The abstract says the model shows possible dynamics. It does not claim history followed them. Only the abstract was read; the publisher site blocked access to the full text.
