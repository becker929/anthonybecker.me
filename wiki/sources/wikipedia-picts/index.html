What it is

This is a encyclopedia entry with no named author. It covers the Picts' name, origins, history, kings, society, religion, art and language. It also lists a related article on the Picts in fantasy.

What it says

  • The Picts were peoples north of the Firth of Forth in the Early Middle Ages. The name Picti is first recorded by outsiders in the late third century.
  • The name is usually read as Latin for painted. The article says evidence for tattooing is limited.
  • The article says the Picts were a heterogeneous group with little cultural uniformity. It warns against treating them as one fixed ethnic people.
  • The kingdom of Fortriu dominated from the seventh century. Pictish and Dal Riata kingdoms merged into the Kingdom of Alba, and Gaelicisation followed.
  • Bede's origin story from Scythia is called pseudohistorical. A 2023 study of eight genomes supports a local origin.
  • Pictish kingship is often called matrilineal. The article says kings were usually followed by brothers or cousins.
  • Pictish was an extinct Insular Celtic language, probably related to Brittonic. The well-known symbols on standing stones are untranslated, and some linguists call the ogham non-Celtic.

What evidence it gives

The article cites many academic books and papers. Examples are Fraser 2009 and Woolf 2017. It also quotes the Annals of Ulster once. It reports a 2023 genome study and archaeology such as hoards and excavations.

Interests and limits
