What it is

The first-person author works on tooling for coding agents. The post is based on a talk given at Y Combinator. It explains the workflow and the reasoning behind it.

What it says

  • The post claims coding agents can work in large codebases with context engineering. It calls the method frequent intentional compaction.
  • The method keeps context use in a 40%-60% range across research, plan and implement steps.
  • The post says the context window is the only lever on output quality. It ranks incorrect information as the worst problem.
  • Subagents are for context control, not for playing roles.
  • Human review is most useful on research and plans. A bad plan line can cause hundreds of bad code lines.
  • The author reports shipping 35k lines to BAML in 7 hours, with a colleague. One pull request was still open.
  • The post reports a failed attempt on parquet-java. Its research steps missed deep dependencies.

What evidence it gives

The evidence is anecdote. The author describes a bug fix on a Rust project that a maintainer approved. The author also describes a large feature pair and one failure. No controlled comparison is given.

Interests and limits

The author's company sells a tool announced at the post's end. It has a stake. The results are self-reported. The 3-5 day estimate came from a colleague. The post's images and videos were not viewed. The post is undated and names no author.
