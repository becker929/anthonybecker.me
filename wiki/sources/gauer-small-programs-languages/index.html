What it is

Dave Gauer writes notes on why tiny programs and small languages appeal. The page is a card in the author's personal collection of notes. It mixes examples, quotations and opinion.

What it says

  • The author says small programs are approachable and more likely to get read.
  • A reader's 46-byte Forth is shown as a hexdump. The author says tiny code feels less scary.
  • Code golf is called meaningful: a Lisp with garbage collection can fit in 436 bytes.
  • Forth, Lisp and Tcl have tiny cores and great power. They demand a shift in thinking.
  • The author names Lua as small yet approachable, with a core described in 27 pages.
  • The author quotes David Ungar that simplicity beats expressiveness.
  • The author ties the love of tiny code to a wider love of miniatures.

What evidence it gives

The page lists examples such as a 25-line library and a 122-line interpreter. It shows a 46-byte hexdump and a short Lisp evaluator. It quotes Ungar, Garfield and Langin-Hooper. It gives no measurements.

Interests and limits

The page is one person's opinion and taste. Comparisons of language size, such as Lua against Zig, are not measured. The page does give a short counterpoint on expressiveness. Quotations from other writers were not checked.
