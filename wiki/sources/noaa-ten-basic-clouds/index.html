What it is

NOAA is a United States government agency. The page is part of its JetStream weather learning site. It aims to teach readers the standard cloud types.

What it says

  • The page defines ten basic cloud types, using definitions from the WMO International Cloud Atlas.
  • Cirrus, cirrocumulus and cirrostratus are the high-level clouds, and are typically thin and white.
  • Altocumulus, altostratus and nimbostratus are mid-level clouds, mainly water droplets, but ice at low temperatures.
  • Cumulus, stratocumulus, stratus and cumulonimbus are low-level clouds, though cumulonimbus extends into the high level.
  • Cirrus is always ice crystals. It is often yellow or red near sunrise and sunset.
  • Nimbostratus is often wrongly called low-level because its base lowers as precipitation continues.
  • The page says the ten types grew from Luke Howard's observation that clouds mix categories.

What evidence it gives

The page relies on the WMO International Cloud Atlas as its authority. It offers definitions and descriptions, not data or experiments.
