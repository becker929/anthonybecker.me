What it is

Emily Temple, managing editor at Lit Hub, wrote the list. It picks stories that became cultural shorthand rather than the best ones. A second list suggests stories that deserve more fame.

What it says

  • Temple defines iconic as a story that has entered general cultural consciousness.
  • Temple says icon status is highly subjective and tied to American culture.
  • Film adaptations and catchy, reworkable titles help a story become iconic.
  • Raymond Carver's title is cited for spawning many echoes, from Murakami to Englander.
  • Temple counts some novellas, such as The Turn of the Screw, as stories.
  • Temple concedes the main list is too white and male and adds a second list.

What evidence it gives

The page is a list with one short blurb per entry. Blurbs cite adaptations, a Vonnegut quote and personal anecdotes. It gives no reader data. Its few counts, such as 17 film adaptations, come from Wikipedia.

Interests and limits

Lit Hub may earn a commission on books linked from the page. The selection is one editor's taste and covers American readers only. No method is given for choosing the stories. The page is a list, not an argument.
