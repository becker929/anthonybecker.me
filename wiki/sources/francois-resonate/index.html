What it is

Alexandre R.J. François, the algorithm's developer, wrote the page. It describes Resonate and an extension where resonators track frequency. The page also covers synthesis, publications and software.

What it says

  • The page says each resonator updates per sample, with no buffering.
  • The page says cost grows linearly with the number of resonators.
  • The page says resonators can tune themselves to follow the frequencies in the input.
  • The page says Resonate gives finer time resolution than FFT-based spectrograms.
  • The page says the dense output can synthesize audio, with pitch and speed changes.
  • The page says a live demo used 112 resonators, drawing a slice every 1.46 milliseconds.
  • The page lists a Best Paper Award for the 2025 Resonate paper.

What evidence it gives

The page gives update formulas and side-by-side spectrograms against the constant-Q transform. It embeds audio of songs shifted in pitch and speed. It links to two papers and open source code. It claims real-time performance but gives no timing figures.

Interests and limits

The page is the developer's own, and it links a Resonate Store. Comparisons are shown as images, with no error measures. Performance claims are not backed by figures here. The linked papers were not read for this page.
