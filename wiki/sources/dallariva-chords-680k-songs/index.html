What it is

Chris Dalla Riva writes the newsletter Can't Get Much Higher, about music and data. This issue explores a dataset with no prior hypothesis.

What it says

  • The name Chordonomicon was coined by 5 researchers last October. It covers chords and genres of nearly 680,000 songs.
  • Dalla Riva had no hypothesis and wanted only to see what the dataset held.
  • The dataset has nearly 52 million notated chords.
  • G major and C major top the chord counts. Together they make up 24% of all chords.
  • Chord choices differ across genres, which the post calls interesting.

What evidence it gives

The preview shows a chart of the 20 most common chords. They come from nearly 52 million chords. Analysis of progressions, which the title promises, is not in the preview.
