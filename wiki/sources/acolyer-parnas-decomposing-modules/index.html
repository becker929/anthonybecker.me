What it is

Adrian Colyer writes The Morning Paper, a blog about computer science research. This post summarises a paper by David Parnas from 1971. It asks whether the lessons apply to services today.

What it says

  • Colyer says a modularization's success depends on the criteria used to divide the system.
  • The post says Parnas hoped modules would cut development time, add flexibility and aid comprehension.
  • The first design makes each processing step a module, with shared data.
  • The second design follows information hiding, so each module hides one design decision.
  • The post says many changes force edits in every module of the first design.
  • The post quotes Parnas warning the second design may be much less efficient.
  • Parnas concludes that starting from a flowchart is almost always incorrect.

What evidence it gives

The post offers one worked example, a small index generator treated as a large project. It relies on Parnas's subjective judgement about comprehensibility. It reports no measurements.

Interests and limits

The post summarises a paper and does not test its claims. Colyer applies the ideas to microservices as an analogy, with no data. Parnas calls the example somewhat of a toy.
