What it is

The thread follows a page by Nicolae Vartolomei, dated 2017 and updated in 2022. The page lists influential papers. Commenters suggest omissions, correct framing and branch into side topics.

What it says

  • The top comment says such lists omit Armstrong's PhD thesis; replies debate whether it belongs.
  • One commenter offers deeper cuts, including chain replication, Paxos Made Live and PBFT.
  • That commenter says the CAP formalization led many to choose eventual consistency too readily.
  • The same commenter says a chain replication variant runs inside several AWS products.
  • A long subthread argues whether systems need one global event order or only partial orders.
  • Commenters add reading lists, including an MIT course that updates its readings yearly.
  • A commenter who left WhatsApp in 2019 thinks its stack is mostly still Erlang.

What evidence it gives

Comments cite papers, courses and blog posts by name. Most points are expert opinion. Some side threads, such as the physics of computation, are speculation.

Interests and limits

Comments are unverified, and several claims about companies come from unnamed internal knowledge. Long stretches wander to unrelated topics, such as prodigies. The linked list is short and gives papers without commentary.
