What it is

User gmays posted the BBC article's headline. The thread discusses the birth, its cost, safety and ethics, and then many side topics.

What it says

  • Commenters discussed a quoted cost of about £30,000 per transplant and compared US prices.
  • One commenter noted the case involved MRKH syndrome, where the womb is missing or underdeveloped.
  • Some questioned the safety of anti-rejection drugs for the baby.
  • A long branch debated the UK Supreme Court ruling on the word woman.
  • One commenter quoted a BBC passage: the first such birth was in Sweden in 2014.
  • Commenters cited an essay on pregnancy as an evolutionary conflict between mother and fetus.
  • Others speculated about artificial wombs and about transplants for trans women.

What evidence it gives

Commenters offer opinion, personal anecdote and a few links to articles. A few figures come from a BBC link a commenter posted. Nobody in the thread presents original data.

Interests and limits

Commenters are anonymous and many claims are unchecked. The linked BBC article was not opened; only the thread was read. Large parts concern gender definitions, birds, cloning and etymology, not the transplant. One medical claim on childbirth was challenged by another commenter.
