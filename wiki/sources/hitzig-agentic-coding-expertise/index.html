What it is

Zoe Hitzig and five co-authors wrote this Anthropic research report. It studies interactive Claude Code sessions from October 2025 to April 2026. It sets out a framework for what work is done and whether it succeeds.

What it says

  • People make about 70% of planning decisions and 20% of execution decisions.
  • Expert-rated sessions set off 12 actions per prompt, against about five for novices.
  • Novice-rated sessions reach verified success 15% of the time, against 28-33% for intermediate and above.
  • In troubled sessions, verified success rises from 4% for novices to 15% for experts.
  • Troubled novice sessions end abandoned 19% of the time, against 5-7% for others.
  • In code-producing sessions, the ten largest occupations land within seven points of software engineers.
  • The share of sessions spent fixing broken code fell from 33% to 19%.

What evidence it gives

Claude Sonnet 4.6 classifiers label about 400,000 sessions from about 235,000 people. Comparisons hold work mode, task value, month, subject and occupation group fixed. Over 90% of sessions labelled as code-creating showed code changes in telemetry. Success is judged from transcripts, using signals such as commits and passing tests.

Interests and limits

Anthropic wrote the report about its own product. The authors call the findings preliminary. Real-world outcomes, such as whether code was used, are not observed. All labels depend on a model reading transcripts. Headless and third-party usage is excluded. The main report was read in full, but the linked appendix was not fetched.
