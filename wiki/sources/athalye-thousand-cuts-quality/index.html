What it is

Aditya Athalye writes a personal blog. The essay asks what software quality is and who owns it. The author calls it a work in progress and not novel.

What it says

  • The author defines quality as the experience of the process, done with grace.
  • Software is pure concept, and most of it must change because the world does.
  • The author suggests quality assurance may belong to every function, not one group.
  • A strictly linear workflow front-loads risk at analysis, and delayed feedback makes errors costly.
  • Every hotfix, complaint, crash and outage is a cut that slowly destroys value.
  • The author lists quality-destroying habits, such as treating testing as quality assurance.
  • No best-practice process can fix broken systems and people, so the way must be co-evolved.

What evidence it gives

The essay offers argument, experience and a hand-drawn ASCII diagram of cost against time. It lists example scenarios and quality-destroying phrases the author has heard. It gives no measurements or studies of its own.

Interests and limits

This is one practitioner's intuition, which the author says is a work in progress. No data supports the claims. The enterprise and consumer product table is mostly left unfinished. The reading list is a sampler, not an argument.
