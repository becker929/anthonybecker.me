What it is

Carla Ra, a sci-fi writer, wrote this post for a writing audience. It explains each of the four acts. It then maps two examples onto the acts.

What it says

  • Kishotenketsu is a four-act structure common in Asian storytelling: introduction, development, twist and conclusion.
  • The post says the structure, not the story, lacks conflict.
  • The twist changes the plot's direction and shows the story in a new light.
  • A four-line poem by Sanyo Rai is read as one verse per act.
  • The film Your Name is analyzed act by act, with spoilers.
  • After the twist, the post says, the romance becomes secondary to saving the town.

What evidence it gives

The post gives two worked examples, a poem and one film. It cites Wikipedia for the poem. It offers no scholarship on the form.

Interests and limits

The author is a fiction writer. The page promotes a story, an anthology and a newsletter. The post carries a sponsored tag and a sticker plug. It is a four-minute read and applies the form to only two works.
