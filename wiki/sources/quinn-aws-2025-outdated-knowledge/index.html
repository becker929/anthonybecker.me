What it is

Corey Quinn writes about AWS costs and runs a newsletter and podcasts. This post gathers changes in core services that Quinn and readers noticed.

What it says

  • Quinn says AWS is almost twenty years old, so older knowledge is often stale.
  • Quinn says EC2 now allows security group and IAM role changes without a shutdown.
  • Quinn says S3 is now read-after-write consistent and no longer needs randomized key prefixes.
  • Quinn says Glacier is now an S3 storage class and restores are no longer slow.
  • Quinn says public IPv4 addresses are no longer free.
  • Quinn says Lambda now allows 15-minute runs, container images and up to 10GB of memory.
  • Quinn says EC2 bills by the second, and Savings Plans are replacing Reserved Instances.

What evidence it gives

The post offers assertions from the author's experience and reader tips. It shows no measurements. Its claims can be checked against AWS documentation.

Interests and limits

The author works for a firm that helps companies cut AWS bills. The claims were not verified here. The list reflects AWS as of the post date. Some items are the author's opinion, such as trust in advisory tools.
