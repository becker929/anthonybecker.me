What it is

Adam Kurkiewicz of Vineyard Finance wrote this post on 8 July 2026. It reports a test of GLM 5.2 preparing a quarterly UK VAT return. GLM 5.2 is an open-weights AI model. The post explains the method, scoring and errors, then argues for AI bookkeeping.

What it says

  • The post says GLM 5.2 processed 59 transactions in 68 minutes for $2.73.
  • The net VAT position was off by 7 pence from the human-prepared return.
  • Of 354 scored checks, the model failed 20 across 18 transactions.
  • The one serious error put 10,000 GBP of founder share capital in the wrong account.
  • 14 of the 17 other affected transactions confused zero-rated VAT with tax-exempt.
  • The audit found no overt cheating, though the model remarked on being tested.
  • The post concludes that book-keeping is quickly becoming a solved problem.

What evidence it gives

The benchmark uses one quarter of one company's books, with 59 transactions. Humans prepared and checked the ground truth. Each transaction was scored on six criteria from the final state of the accounting software. A table gives turns, tool calls, tokens and cost by month. The author audited the model's reasoning for cheating.

Interests and limits

The author's firm sells the Toot bookkeeping product, shown on the same site. The test covers one small company and one quarter. Humans did broader work, such as finding the invoices. The cost claim sets raw token cost against typical accountant fees. The post reports one run per month. The post does not score human book-keepers against the same checks.
