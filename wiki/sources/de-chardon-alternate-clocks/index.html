What it is

Cyrille Médard de Chardon's page shows working clocks based on different divisions of the day. They include 24-hour, decimal, binary, hexadecimal and 36-hour versions. The page asks whether a better time system could exist.

What it says

  • The standard day of 24 hours, 60 minutes and 60 seconds seems arbitrary, it says.
  • A decimal day has 10 hours, each with 100 minutes of 100 seconds.
  • Decimal seconds last 86.4% of a classic second, and conversions become decimal shifts.
  • A binary clock lacks accuracy, since each of its seconds lasts three hours.
  • The 36-hour clock came from a reader's request, and the labels don't quite fit.

What evidence it gives

The evidence is the working clocks themselves. The page makes a preference argument and reports no tests of readability.

Interests and limits

This is a short personal exploration with an old codebase, which the author calls inflexible. Its claim that decimal time is easier to read is a personal judgment.
