What it is

Brittany Ellich writes a personal essay on the mental load. That is the work of remembering what needs doing. The author describes a family vacation and a weekly meal routine, both run with Claude. The essay sets out to persuade people carrying that load to use such tools.

What it says

  • Ellich says the mental load is the noticing and remembering, not the chores themselves.
  • Ellich says one Claude session turned a trip brain dump into checklists and calendar entries.
  • Ellich has Claude plan weekly meals and a self-built tool compile a grocery list.
  • Ellich says partners sharing the tracking is right but slow, while a tool helps today.
  • Ellich argues losing some skills to AI is acceptable. Not every brain capacity is worth defending.
  • Ellich says the freed capacity went into blog posts and a podcast called Overcommitted.
  • Ellich grants that the technology's development is problematic but says rejecting the tool outright oversimplifies.

What evidence it gives

The essay offers the author's own experience. It adds one anecdote about the author's mother using Claude for diet and exercise. It names no measurements, time savings or comparison. It cites a 2017 comic by Emma for the idea of mental load.

Interests and limits

The essay is advocacy built on one household's experience. The tool described is Claude, so the praise is for a named product. The author notes privilege in ordering groceries online. The essay says nothing about cost, errors or what happens when the tools fail. It offers no data on whether skills fade.
