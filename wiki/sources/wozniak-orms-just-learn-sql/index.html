What it is

An unnamed author who builds report-heavy software on event data wrote this. The post explains why the author came to prefer plain SQL over object-relational mappers.

What it says

  • The author concludes that, for the author, ORMs are more detriment than benefit.
  • Wide tables make ORM queries fetch every attribute; a projection cut one query to seconds.
  • The author reports a table with over 600 attributes and 14 joins per object.
  • The author says fast ORM queries are often more obscure than SQL itself.
  • The author says schemas end up in two places, and ORMs do not help migrations.
  • The author describes tables in the ORM but writes queries with a templating system.
  • The author describes treating queries as the API to the database.

What evidence it gives

The post offers personal experience and a few specific figures from past projects. Examples include a query sped up from minutes to seconds. It also cites an essay by Neward on the object/relational mismatch.

Interests and limits

The author says the advice fits event timelines and reports, not every application. The figures cannot be checked from the post. The post compares no ORM against hand-written SQL in a controlled test. The author is considering stored procedures but has not adopted them.
