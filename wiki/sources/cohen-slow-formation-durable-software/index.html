What it is

The author was a historian at George Mason University's Center for History and New Media. The post marks the tool's 20th anniversary. It sets out to explain how the team arrived at the idea.

What it says

  • The post says Zotero took five years to reach a rough prototype.
  • The post argues AI could not have sped this up. The team lacked a clear aim.
  • The team wanted a tool combining Scribe's research features with Web Scrapbook's browser capture.
  • Firefox, released in November 2004, let the team extend the browser through XUL.
  • The name moved from SmartFox to Firefox Scholar, then to zotero, an Albanian word.
  • The post reports 60,000 users within a month of the first beta.

What evidence it gives

The post quotes dated emails from 2003 and 2004, plus a grant abstract. It names team members and shows screenshots. The screenshots were not viewed. The AI claim is a counterfactual with no test.

Interests and limits

The author co-directed the project and says the account is from that perspective. The author shows pride in the project. The claim about AI is speculation. The post does not claim to be a full history.
