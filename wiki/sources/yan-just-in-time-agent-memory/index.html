What it is

The authors work at the Beijing Academy of Artificial Intelligence. The paper contrasts Ahead-of-Time memory, built before a request, with Just-In-Time memory. It introduces JAM, the Memory-Gym training data, and a two-stage training method.

What it says

  • The authors say memory built ahead of time can discard fine-grained details that later matter.
  • JAM has a Memorizer and a Researcher. The Memorizer keeps raw sessions as files in folders with README summaries.
  • The Researcher uses open, search and browse tools in a loop. It stops when it judges the evidence sufficient.
  • Memory-Gym synthesizes training data with nine task types across six domains.
  • The authors report that a 4B-parameter JAM outperforms 7B to 14B trained baselines. This holds across most settings.
  • On LongCodeQA, a domain outside the training data, accuracy rose from 54.08% to 75.54%.

What evidence it gives

The paper reports tables on four benchmarks against ten baselines. It adds ablations, timing and a human audit of 200 training instances. LoCoMo overall F1 was 52.09 for JAM against 43.37 for RAG. Code is released.

Interests and limits

The authors built JAM, Memory-Gym and the comparisons, so results are self-reported. The paper is a preprint. JAM adds query-time latency over one-shot systems. The paper says other memory systems stay competitive on dialogue benchmarks such as LoCoMo. The main text was read, but the appendices were only skimmed.
