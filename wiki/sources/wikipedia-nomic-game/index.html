What it is

The article is an encyclopedia entry on Nomic, written by Wikipedia contributors. It covers the name, the gameplay, online play and variants. It sets out to describe the game, not to argue a position.

What it says

  • Nomic is a 1982 game by Peter Suber with votes on rule changes.
  • Suber used it to suggest rule-changing systems can end up with contradictory laws.
  • In the original rules, each turn has a proposal, a vote and a die roll.
  • Rules are mutable or immutable, and immutable ones must be transmuted before they change.
  • The original win condition is 100 points, which Suber called deliberately boring.
  • BlogNomic ends each dynasty when someone wins, repealing every rule except the core ones.
  • Variants include Nomic combined with Monopoly or chess, and Nomyx, which is computerized.

What evidence it gives

It cites Suber pages from 2003 and 1990 and a 1985 Hofstadter chapter. It names live games such as Agora, running since 1993, and BlogNomic. It offers no data or tests.
