Mathematical Patterns in African American Hairstyles

A short paper on tessellation patterns in African American hair braiding, with teaching ideas.

What it is

Gloria Gilmer is a mathematician and mathematics educator. The paper was prepared for a National Council of Teachers of Mathematics meeting. It sets out to show the mathematics in a cultural practice.

What it says

  • The paper says braiders use tessellations without seeing them as mathematics.
  • It says only the regular hexagon, square and equilateral triangle tessellate among regular polygons.
  • It says box braids follow a brick-wall pattern.
  • It says triangular braids drew hair to the point where the angle bisectors meet.
  • It says box braids hide the scalp and, the authors were told, move little.
  • The author asks what braiding can give mathematics education, and what education can give braiding.

What evidence it gives

The evidence is the author's observation of stylists and customers in salons. Two collaborators also observed and interviewed stylists. No counts of salons or styles are given. The figures were not available in the text, so only the written descriptions were read.

Interests and limits

The paper is short and descriptive, with no measurements or study design. The page carries no date. A date of 12/31/98 appears only in the hosting site footer. The figures were not viewed. The paper aims at teachers and is not a formal study.
