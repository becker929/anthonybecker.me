What it is

An essay by Frank Lantz on Donkeyspace. It follows a podcast talk about Network Wars, a small strategy game. It asks what the bots in that game really are.

What it says

  • Lantz says the bots follow simple, predictable rules. Players can steer them into fighting each other.
  • Lantz says the bots are rules, like gravity in Tetris, not opponents.
  • Lantz says even a complex or perfect-playing AI would still be part of the game.
  • Lantz suggests all game AI opponents are best seen as internal features.
  • Lantz concludes you are a player only while developing a strategy. Executing one does not count.

What evidence it gives

The essay rests on the author's own play and the game's rules. It also uses thought experiments, such as a Blackjack cheat sheet. It gives no tests or data. Two comments offer a quotation and a layered-games model.

Interests and limits

The post and two comments were read in full. Six more comments were not shown on the page. The argument rests on one simple game. The author admits some bot behavior is not fully worked out.
