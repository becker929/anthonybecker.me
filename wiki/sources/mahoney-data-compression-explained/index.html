What it is

Matt Mahoney wrote the book for readers who want to understand or write compression software.

What it says

  • Mahoney says no universal compression algorithm exists, and random data cannot be compressed.
  • The book says compression is modeling plus coding, and that optimal modeling cannot be computed.
  • Mahoney says optimal compression would solve the AI problem, because prediction relates to understanding.
  • The book explains LZ77 as replacing repeated strings with pointers to earlier occurrences.
  • It calls lossy compression a hard AI problem and cites Landauer's 2 bits per second.
  • The conclusion says the best compressors combine the predictions of many independent models.
  • The book closes with a physics argument that the universe has a finite description length.

What evidence it gives

The book gives proofs and counting arguments, worked examples and C++ code. It also lists results on the Calgary corpus and a large text benchmark. The closing physics argument rests on reasoning and constants, not experiment.

Interests and limits

The book is a single author's account, last updated in 2013. Several claims about AI and physics are the author's own reasoning, not settled results. Only parts of this long book were read. They were the opening, chapter 1, the start of chapter 2 and the conclusion. Samples of chapters 4, 5 and 6 were also read, but chapter 3 was not.
