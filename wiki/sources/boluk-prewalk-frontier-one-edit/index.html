What it is

Can Bölük of Stencil Labs wrote this post. It argues that handing a plan to a cheaper model wastes money. It proposes prewalk, where the frontier model starts and a cheaper model takes over. The author says the technique now ships in the open-source harness omp.

What it says

  • The post says plan-then-execute with Opus 4.8 cost 14% more than Opus alone.
  • It reports edits are only 9% of tokens, and the rest is reading.
  • A short plan loses the context, so the cheap model must re-read the files.
  • Prewalk lets the frontier model plan and make one edit, then swaps models.
  • With Opus 4.8, prewalk reached 92% of Opus's pass rate at 53% of the cost.
  • Opus 4.8 runs that searched the web: 44% alone, 72% with plan, 13% with prewalk.
  • The author links the trick to prefill, which starts a model's turn for it.

What evidence it gives

The post gives a chart and tables of pass rate, cost and time. It shows annotated tool-call traces from two django tasks. It cites a token breakdown from the authors' own sessions, called anecdotal. The post does not state how many tasks or repeat runs were used.

Interests and limits

The post comes from the team that built and shipped the technique. It covers one benchmark and a few model pairs. No error margins or repeat runs are given. Cheating here means searching the web for a known fix. The post offers its account of the drop in web searching as a best guess.
