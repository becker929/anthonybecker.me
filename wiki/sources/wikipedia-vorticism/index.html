What it is

Wikipedia contributors wrote this reference article. It covers Wyndham Lewis's London group, the magazine BLAST, the war years and later reputation.

What it says

  • Wyndham Lewis formed the London-based movement in 1914, partly inspired by Cubism.
  • Ezra Pound's idea of the vortex named the movement. Lewis liked it for being vague.
  • The Vorticist magazine BLAST was launched at a dinner on 15 July 1914.
  • War began weeks later, and the 1915 Vorticist Exhibition was ignored by the press.
  • Within a year of the war's start, most Vorticist artists had enlisted.
  • Thirty-eight of forty-nine works shown in 1915 were later missing, according to Richard Cork.
  • The movement was largely forgotten until a 1956 Tate dispute and Cork's 1974 exhibition.

What evidence it gives

The article cites exhibition catalogues, memoirs and essays in its notes. It quotes the manifesto, Lewis's catalogue foreword and Pound. It includes no original research.

Interests and limits

Wikipedia is open to editing, so the text reflects its contributors at one date. The account leans on Cork's catalogue. It describes style in broad terms and shows few works in detail.
