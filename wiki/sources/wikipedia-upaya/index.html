What it is

A reference article on a Buddhist term. It covers its meaning, role, images, the Pali Canon and modern mindfulness debates.

What it says

  • Upaya means adapting a teaching to the audience. The method need not be ultimately true.
  • The Digital Dictionary of Buddhism says fāngbiàn can mean skillful or expedient. Context decides.
  • The Lotus Sutra tells a burning house parable. A father lures children out with promised carts.
  • Zen teachers use the empty fist image, as in a record of Linji Yixuan.
  • Richard Gombrich says the term is post-canonical but the practice matters in the Pali Canon. The article adds that a Pali form occurs in the Digha Nikaya.
  • Critics such as Ron Purser and David Loy reject commercial mindfulness. Jon Kabat-Zinn is said to have secularized it.

What evidence it gives

The article quotes the Lotus Sutra at length in Burton Watson's translation. It quotes Gombrich, Conze and Gregory. It lists six scholarly works under references.

Interests and limits

This is a general summary with citation gaps in places. Quoted passages are long and the text is the version edited on 1 November 2026. Debates on mindfulness are covered in a few sentences only.
