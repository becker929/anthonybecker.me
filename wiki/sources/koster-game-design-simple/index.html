What it is

Raph Koster is a game designer and the author of A Theory of Fun. The essay is a cheat sheet in twelve steps. Each step ends with a bottom-line sentence. The aim is to show how fun, loops, feedback, pacing and motivation fit together.

What it says

  • Koster says fun is basically about making progress on prediction.
  • Koster says a problem is constraints plus a goal. Rules with no goal make a toy.
  • Koster says games have an operational loop and a progression spiral.
  • Koster says good feedback shows available actions, that one was used, and its effect.
  • Koster says Pac-Man got solved, so Ms. Pac-Man added randomness to escalate.
  • Koster says games nest, web and chain small loops into larger structures.
  • Koster says people learn best when a problem sits just past their current ability.

What evidence it gives

The essay offers argument and worked examples. The examples include a stick-clicking game, Snake, Pac-Man, Tetris and Connect Four. It names theorists such as Nicole Lazzaro, Roger Caillois and Mark LeBlanc. It cites no studies and gives no data.

Interests and limits

The author is a working designer who sells books. The page carries affiliate links to them. The essay calls its own list a strategy guide that others may dispute. The original site refused the download. The text came from a Wayback Machine copy dated 19 October 2026. The essay claims to cover all game types without testing that.
