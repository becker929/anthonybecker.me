What it is

Matthew Burgos wrote the piece for designboom, a design news site. It reports on the maker's autonomous version of an earlier remote-controlled flying umbrella. It describes the frame, the tracking and the build process.

What it says

  • John Tse of I Build Stuff made an autonomous flying umbrella that follows its user. It updates an earlier remote-controlled version.
  • Four propellers sit on arms that fold inward when not in use. The arms lock open with hinges, rubber bands and plates.
  • Tracking went through many versions. These included regular cameras, GPS and a time-of-flight depth camera.
  • A Raspberry Pi finds the person's head in the depth data. It sends commands to the flight controller to stay centered overhead.
  • Most structural parts were 3D printed, some in carbon-fiber nylon.
  • After nearly a year and many failures, it worked. It could hover, follow a person and fly in heavy rain.

What evidence it gives

The article offers description and photographs credited to the maker. It gives no flight times, tracking accuracy or test figures. Its claims about hovering, following and rain flight come from the maker.

Interests and limits

The article relays one maker's own project and video without independent testing. The images and video were not viewed, only the text. It says the umbrella was not perfect but gives no detail. The page also carries advertising and shop listings.
