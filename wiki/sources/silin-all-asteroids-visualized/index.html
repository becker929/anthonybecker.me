What it is

The thread sits under a link to a GitHub project called Orbidium. The poster, d_silin, says a team from Exodus Orbitals wrote the demo. Commenters ask how to read the picture.

What it says

  • The poster says the demo plots all 1.4M asteroids, each as part of its ellipse.
  • The poster says fewer than a hundred asteroids have an apoapsis inside Earth's orbit.
  • One commenter says discoveries cluster in directions facing away from Earth.
  • A commenter argues the Kirkwood gaps should not show up in an accurate simulation.
  • After switching to random orbit positions, the poster says the belt gaps were rendering artifacts.
  • The poster says true positions need ephemeris data. The demo is mostly a visualization.

What evidence it gives

The thread offers argument and the poster's description of the code. A commenter points to a video of discoveries over time. Nobody in the thread publishes measurements.

Interests and limits

The poster belongs to the team that made the demo and links a sign-up list. The linked repository was not read. Two commenters talk past each other on the gaps. One commenter replies that the gaps are a real physical phenomenon. Claims come from commenters and were not checked.
