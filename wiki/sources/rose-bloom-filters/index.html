What it is

Sam Rose writes a tutorial for programmers who know what a hash function is. The post aims to show when a Bloom filter is worth using.

What it says

  • A Bloom filter can give definite no answers, but a yes only means maybe.
  • A Bloom filter never gives a false negative, and the post stresses this.
  • The post says a 20MB list of bad links could fit in 3.59MB.
  • Each item sets bits chosen by hash functions. A full filter claims everything is present.
  • More hash functions lower errors but fill the filter faster and cost more.
  • Formulas give the best bit and hash counts from item count and error rate.
  • Standard filters cannot delete items. Counting variants can, at a cost in memory.

What evidence it gives

The post uses worked examples and short formulas in JavaScript. It gives two real uses, Akamai caching and Google BigTable. It also says Chrome used a Bloom filter until 2012. It offers no benchmarks of its own.

Interests and limits

This is a personal teaching post with no commercial stake apparent. It was read as text only, so the interactive demos were not run. The size figures are illustrations of one scenario. The post says the tool is niche.
