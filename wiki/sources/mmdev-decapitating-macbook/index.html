What it is

The author of the mm-dev site, who is not named, wrote the series. It starts from the need to test apps on Apple hardware. It covers buying, stripping, resetting and remotely running the machine. The page combines all the parts into one view.

What it says

  • The author says building for Apple platforms needs Apple hardware, and virtual machines were joyless.
  • The author bought a smashed-screen M1 MacBook Air for 300 pounds and removed the display.
  • The login UI sits on the broken screen; a shortcut moves it to the monitor.
  • Telling whether the machine is on proved hard, so the author scripted a ping.
  • Magnets in the detached lid triggered the base's sensors and made it sleep and wake.
  • In daily use, the Mac builds Android APKs over SSH and is reached by NoMachine.
  • The author likes the silence and efficiency but reports the battery drains when switched off.

What evidence it gives

The series is a first-person account with shell scripts and notes on what happened. It offers no measurements. Power and charging advice is admitted to be a hunch.

Interests and limits

This is one person's experience with one machine, and the author is not named. Some advice is flagged as a guess, such as cable plugging order. The photos and screenshots on the page were not viewed.
