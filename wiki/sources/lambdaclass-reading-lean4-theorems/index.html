What it is

The LambdaClass blog published this guide. It aims to teach readers to read proofs. The post says AI can generate Lean code, but people must still read the theorems.

What it says

  • A theorem is a function with a type signature, and a proof is its body.
  • Lean's small kernel checks every proof, so buggy tactics cannot slip an invalid one through.
  • A dozen tactics, such as intro, apply, rw, simp and omega, cover most proofs.
  • Readers should start with the theorem statement, not the proof.
  • Big theorems are not necessarily hard to prove but hard to state, per the post.
  • The kernel checks what was stated, not what was meant.

What evidence it gives

The post gives about a dozen code examples. They come from AMO-Lean and OptiSat, which the post calls open-source verified projects. The post says all examples compile with Lean 4. It offers no user study or measurements.

Interests and limits

LambdaClass publishes the post, and the post does not say who maintains the projects. The post does not test whether readers learn from it. Its claims about the kernel are stated, not demonstrated. It covers reading proofs, not writing them.
