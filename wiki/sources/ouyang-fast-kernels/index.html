What it is

Anne Ouyang, Azalia Mirhoseini and Percy Liang report an early result. They set out to make synthetic data for training kernel models. Test-time search alone produced fast kernels, so they published early.

What it says

  • AI-generated CUDA-C kernels matched or beat PyTorch's expert-tuned kernels on some tasks.
  • Reported speeds on FP32 problems ranged from 101.3% for matmul to 484.4% for LayerNorm.
  • Each round writes optimization ideas in plain language. Each idea spawns several kernel variants, and the best seed the next round.
  • The post says sequential revision loops often stall on the same kinds of change.
  • In the Conv2D example, speed rose from 20.1% of PyTorch to 179.9% over 13 rounds.
  • Most best kernels appeared late, in round 4 or 5 of 5 rounds.
  • Weaker results: FP16 matmul reached 52% and FP16 Flash Attention 9% of PyTorch.

What evidence it gives

The post gives timings for five kernels on one L40S GPU. It adds one step-by-step Conv2D trajectory. Correctness is checked on many random inputs within a 1e-02 tolerance. The full Conv2D code is in the appendix.

Interests and limits

The authors are Stanford researchers, supported by Standard Kernel Company and Prime Intellect. Results come from 10 KernelBench level 1 problems on one GPU, with modified sizes. The post says FP32 is less optimized, which may explain the wins. Gains hold for specific problem sizes. The setup allows lower-precision kernels within the 1e-02 tolerance. The authors call the search budget quite limited.
