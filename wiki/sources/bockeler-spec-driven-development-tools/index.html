What it is

Birgitta Böckeler is a Distinguished Engineer at Thoughtworks. The article is part of a series called Exploring Gen AI. It defines the term, describes three tools, and lists observations and questions.

What it says

  • Böckeler separates three levels. They are spec-first, spec-anchored and spec-as-source.
  • The article finds Kiro too heavy for a small bug. The bug became 4 user stories with 16 acceptance criteria.
  • With spec-kit, Böckeler found many repetitive markdown files. The author would rather review code.
  • The article reports the agent often ignored instructions. In one case it regenerated existing classes as duplicates.
  • Böckeler says Tessl alone aims at spec-anchored work and explores spec-as-source. Generated code still varied between runs.
  • The article compares spec-as-source to model-driven development. That never took off for business applications.

What evidence it gives

The evidence is the author's own hands-on trials, mostly in September. Examples include one small bug in Kiro and one feature in spec-kit. The Tessl trial reverse engineered one JavaScript file. The article says it is not a close-to-real evaluation.

Interests and limits

Böckeler works at Thoughtworks, which publishes tools commentary and sells services. The trials were few and small. Spec-kit's feature was never finished. Tessl was in private beta, and the article says the tools change fast. The article says it lacks long-term usage reports.
