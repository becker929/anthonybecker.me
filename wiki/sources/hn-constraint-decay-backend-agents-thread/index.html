What it is

Posted by wek, the thread links to arXiv paper 2605.06445. The paper tests LLM coding agents on back end code under structural rules. A commenter says it covers tasks across eight web frameworks. This page describes the thread, not the paper.

What it says

  • A commenter quotes the abstract: agent performance drops under explicit architectural rules.
  • Commenter spacedoutman says the strongest model tested, GPT-5.2, is nearly six months old.
  • Commenter mkozlows says the paper used open-source harnesses, not the Codex harness.
  • Commenters suggest example files and architecture linters over prose style rules.
  • Commenter bob1029 reports the opposite: patches grow more accurate in bigger legacy codebases.
  • Commenter phrotoma asks whether constraint decay is just context rot.
  • Commenter pron says agents also struggle to tell when a design rule should change.

What evidence it gives

The thread is opinion and anecdote from self-selected commenters. It quotes lines from the paper's abstract. Commenters describe the paper's setup, models and harnesses, and may err. Several report their own results with coding agents.

Interests and limits

The paper itself was not read for this page. Descriptions of it come from commenters and may be wrong. One commenter says frontier models were not fully tested, for cost reasons. Commenters are self-selected, and many hold strong views on AI. Reports of personal results are not controlled tests.
