What it is

This is a encyclopedia entry with no named author. It describes Loango from about the 16th to the 19th centuries. It sits in what is now the Republic of the Congo, southern Gabon and Cabinda.

What it says

  • The article says Loango's origins are obscure, with its earliest documentary mention around 1561. A Dutch account says the founder came from Kakongo and built the capital at Buali.
  • Loango exported copper to Europe and was a major producer and exporter of cloth. Thousands of meters of its cloth went to Luanda.
  • A rule of succession said to date to Njimbe rotated the throne among four provinces. The provinces were Kaye, Boke, Selage and Kabango.
  • By 1766 French missionaries found no clear succession and long interregna. After King Buatu died in 1787, no king was elected for over a century.
  • In theory the kings held absolute and even divine power. They ruled through appointed governors, with taxes on people, land and livestock.
  • Christian missions came repeatedly, and a king was baptized in 1663. The article says Loango never had a permanent state-sponsored church.
  • European ships visited but set up no permanent factories. Officials called mafouks managed trade in the royal interest.

What evidence it gives

The article cites printed sources, mostly European travellers and missionaries. It also cites modern historians, especially Phyllis Martin. It has numbered footnotes.
