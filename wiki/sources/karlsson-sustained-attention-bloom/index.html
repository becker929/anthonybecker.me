What it is

Henrik Karlsson writes the Escaping Flatland newsletter. The essay aims to show sustained attention is vivid and pleasurable, not just a duty. It draws on seduction, meditation and art.

What it says

  • Karlsson says sustained attention with delayed satisfaction can intensify desire. Karlsson ties this loosely to dopamine and expectation.
  • The essay claims body systems update at different speeds. Frequent switching leaves attention residue.
  • Karlsson says anxiety and joy can both loop, giving a panic attack or jhana states.
  • The essay says other focuses, such as an octopus or loneliness, may also bloom.
  • Karlsson says good art is not about communicating a message. It offers patterns that structure consciousness under sustained attention.
  • The essay describes a concert where Karlsson imagined the music as several films.

What evidence it gives

The essay gives personal accounts, quoted writers and footnotes. These include a jhana retreat report, Darwin on an octopus and Chris Olah. It cites no studies for its mechanism. A footnote mentions an unnamed study of 23 minutes to refocus. It admits being unsure of the exact mechanics.

Interests and limits

This is one writer's reflection with a loose scientific gloss. The essay says it is unsure of the mechanics and the timings. The newsletter is funded by paying subscribers. The first section discusses sexual arousal in non-graphic terms. The essay was read in full, but only two reader comments were loaded.
