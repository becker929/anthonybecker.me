What it is

Sebastian writes for Prime Intellect, a company that trains and hosts models. The post explains the scaffold and reports early ablations.

What it says

  • Long inputs hurt models, so the scaffold delegates input to Python and sub-LLMs.
  • Only sub-LLMs get tools, and the answer must go in a variable.
  • With GPT-5-mini the scaffold tended to raise reward, but not on math-python.
  • On DeepDive it was worse unless told to split questions among sub-LLMs.
  • On real Oolong data the scaffold beat the plain model by a large margin.
  • Open models varied: GLM 4.6 nearly doubled on DeepDive, but tips hurt it.
  • The authors expect training with the scaffold to unlock larger gains.

What evidence it gives

The post reports charts over 50 samples per setting, with reproduction commands. Results cover four environments and four models. The authors say they did not tune any settings.

Interests and limits

Prime Intellect sells training products and calls this scaffold a major research focus. Samples are small, at 50 per setting, and the authors call some patterns likely random. The charts and images were not viewed, only the text. The authors say absolute scores are not the point. On Oolong, the plain model's API rejected long inputs, and those scored zero.
