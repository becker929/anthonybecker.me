What it is

Marcin Wichary writes Unsung, a blog about software craft and quality. The post defines a molly guard and gives examples in hardware and software. It then proposes the reverse idea.

What it says

  • Wichary defines a molly guard as a plastic cover over a significant button.
  • The name allegedly comes from an engineer's daughter who pressed a big red button.
  • Software has molly guards too. Examples are are-you-sure dialogs and the Ctrl and Alt keys.
  • A reverse molly guard is a button that presses itself if the user waits.
  • The post says this matters for long jobs like OS updates. They can stall overnight on a question.
  • Wichary suggests signposting flows so people know when they can walk away with confidence.

What evidence it gives

The post gives argument and a handful of examples shown in images. One named example is Ctrl+Alt+Del. The source of the name is told as an anecdote. There are no studies.

Interests and limits

The post is a short personal observation, with no product for sale. The mobile pattern it mentions is shown only in images. The follow-up post, More molly guards, was not read. The text was read in full, but photos and screenshots were not viewed.
