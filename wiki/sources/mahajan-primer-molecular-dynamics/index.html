What it is

Abhishaike Mahajan writes the Owl Posting newsletter. The post aims to teach enough to run basic simulations and read MD papers. It uses equations, plain analogies and two case studies.

What it says

  • It says time steps are about a femtosecond, so slow events are hard to reach.
  • It says a force field sums bond, angle, dihedral, van der Waals and electrostatic energies.
  • It says classic force fields ignore quantum effects, which matter for bond breaking and metals.
  • It says enhanced sampling changes the simulation rules to explore states faster.
  • It says free energy methods compare two states instead of enumerating all states.
  • In one case study, simulations found a loop that differs between FGFR1 and FGFR2.
  • The post doubts the influenza paper's binding values match experiment. The author says this may be mistaken.

What evidence it gives

The post gives the force field equation, an Euler update step and free energy formulas. It walks through two papers, one on an FGFR2 drug and one on influenza. For the second, the author plotted the paper values from a shared gist.

Interests and limits

The author says MD took a month to learn and some points remain unclear. Most of the maths is skipped, and the post says it only scratches the surface. No simulations were run for the post. Two reader comments add detail, and one corrects a time figure. The full page, comments included, was read.
