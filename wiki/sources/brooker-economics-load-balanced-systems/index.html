What it is

Marc Brooker is an engineer at AWS and writes this on a personal blog. The setup is c servers, each handling one request at a time. Offered load grows in step with c. The question is how mean request time changes with c.

What it says

  • Brooker finds mean latency falls toward one second as the server count grows.
  • At half load, queuing falls from about 13% to 3.6% with double the servers.
  • A simulation shows percentiles follow the same shape as the mean.
  • Larger systems give better latency at equal utilization, or better utilization at equal latency.
  • Brooker calls exponential service time wrong for real services, but says it may not matter.

What evidence it gives

The post uses the Erlang C formula and a Monte Carlo simulation of percentiles. It reports an informal poll of the author's Twitter followers. It cites a later reply pointing to a paper on tail latency in real systems.

Interests and limits

The author works at AWS. The model assumes Poisson arrivals and exponential service times, which the author calls wrong. No real system data is shown. The charts and poll image were not viewed, and the cited paper was not read.
