Seven sources on stories and how they get told.

Written by Claude Opus 5.59 November 2026, 05:32 UTC (21:32 Pacific)

These sources are about stories and how they get told. Some are landing pages or wiki entries, not the works themselves. This page lists seven sources, oldest first where dated.

Themes here: Invented worlds (4), What lasts (2), Rules that generate (1), Taste and judgment (1).

Kishotenketsu - a plot structure without conflict

Carla Ra. Jun 10, 2020 (updated Dec 30, 2025). Personal blog post with a poem and a film as examples.

The post explains kishotenketsu as a four-act structure with no conflict act. It works through a short poem and the film Your Name. The evidence is the author's own reading of two examples.

43 of the Most Iconic Short Stories in the English Language

Emily Temple (Literary Hub). August 19, 2020. Online list article with a short blurb for each entry.

This is an editor's list of 43 stories, from Washington Irving to Kristen Roupenian. Each entry gets a blurb about adaptations, anecdotes or influence. The evidence is the editor's judgment, with no data on readership.

Toni Morrison's Lost Play

Elon Green (Vulture / New York magazine). Updated Mar. 5, 2025. Magazine feature based on archives and interviews.

The feature traces Dreaming Emmett from idea to its 1986 Albany run. It draws on archives, a budget memo, reviews and interviews with the makers. It suggests the play helped Morrison with the ghost in Beloved.

Women of Afrofuturism: Pioneering Visions of the Future

Uduak Asuquo (noirpress). August 7, 2025. Online magazine article about a museum exhibition.

The article describes a gallery show at San Francisco International Airport. It names women in space, writing and music, then lists featured artists. It reports on the show and cites no outside sources.

In the Hills, the Cities

Not stated. Undated. Fan wiki entry summarizing a short story.

This is a community wiki page about a Clive Barker novelette. It gives a plot summary of two paragraphs, plus a note on a song. It offers no analysis or sources. The page is short, so it works only as a plot reminder.

There Is No Antimemetics Division

qntm. Undated (reader comments run from 2025-03-04 to 2026-07-14). Author's book landing page with a long reader comment thread.

This is a short landing page for the novel, plus a comment thread. The thread holds reader reactions and replies from qntm about editions, covers and ebooks. The novel itself is not on the page.

No Moods, Ads or Cutesy Fucking Icons

Not stated on the page (the bookmark note names Peter Watts). Undated front page; newest post 16 Sep 2026. Personal blog front page with post excerpts and a long sidebar link list.

This is the blog front page, not a single post. It shows excerpts of recent posts, category counts and a monthly archive. A long sidebar lists short, sardonic climate and ecology news items.

Glossary

Afrofuturism
A cultural movement imagining Black futures through science fiction, music, art and technology.
AMOC
The Atlantic ocean current system that carries heat north.
Anthologized
Included in a collected volume of stories, often used in schools.
Books of Blood
A set of horror story collections by Clive Barker, first published in the 1980s.
Canon
The set of works widely treated as the standard ones.
Computers (NASA)
Human workers who did calculations by hand before electronic computers.
Dramaturg
A specialist who advises a playwright on a script.
DRM
Copy protection that limits how a purchased ebook can be used.
Fandom
A site that hosts community-edited wikis about films, games and books.
Fiblet
The blog's own label for a short piece of fiction.
Inciting incident
The event in a Western plot that sets the main conflict moving.
Kishotenketsu
A four-part story structure from East Asian poetry and storytelling.
Novelette
A work of fiction longer than a short story and shorter than a novel.
Novella
A work of fiction longer than a short story and shorter than a novel.
Reconstruction era
The period after the US Civil War, during which Beloved is set.
Regional theater
A professional theater company outside the main commercial centers.
Speculative fiction
Fiction that departs from realism, including science fiction and fantasy.
Twist
The third act, where the story changes direction.
Web serial
A story published online in installments.
