Occult, myth and religion

18 sources on magical traditions, ancient myth and religious ideas.

Written by Claude Opus 5.59 November 2026, 17:53 UTC (09:53 Pacific)

These sources cover magical traditions, ancient myth and religious ideas. Many are reference articles, and some are practitioners' own essays. Pages report what each source claims, not whether it is true. This page lists 18 sources, oldest first where dated.

Themes here: Belief at work (9), How forms evolve (3), What lasts (1), What counts as a mind (1), Invented worlds (1).

The Key of It All (AL=L)

George T. Mortimer. 1996, with a foreword added in 2016. Self-published occult monograph on letter-number ciphers, in PDF.

This is a numerological monograph from the 1990s with a sceptical 2016 foreword. It builds on the earlier keys of Charles Stansfeld Jones and Gerald Suster. Its evidence is arithmetic on letter values. The author later calls the results merely intriguing.

Liber Q: Tarot Symbolism & Divination

Revised and expanded by GH Fra. P.; introduction signed James A. Eshelman; College of Thelema. Fourth revised edition, June 2012. Thelemic teaching manual on the Tarot, in PDF.

The PDF is a long reference and training text, not an essay. It revises a Golden Dawn manuscript called Book T. It gives each card a Qabalistic reading and a meditation schedule. The last chapter teaches two divination procedures.

Hru and Hua: A Secret Egregore of the A.A. and H.O.G.D.?

FraterP (Frater Praemonstro), Order of the Thelemic Golden Dawn. 10 April 2020 (updated 15 April 2020). Occult essay on a practitioner's order website, openly speculative.

The essay follows two spirit names through Enochian magic, the Golden Dawn and Crowley. It proposes they point to a single hidden figure linked to Horus. The author calls the idea speculative and the evidence sparse.

User:Tomispev/Coffin Texts Spell 714

Wiktionary user Tomispev. Last edited 11 February 2023. Wiktionary user draft, with translations by Allen and Faulkner.

The page is a short working draft. It sets one ancient Egyptian spell beside the English of two scholars. The spell is a creation speech in which the speaker made itself. The page adds almost no commentary of its own.

Hymn to Babylon, missing for a millennium, has been discovered

Ludwig Maximilian University of Munich (press material), edited by Stephanie Baum, on Phys.org. 1 July 2025. University press release republished as a science news article.

The item reports a paper in the journal Iraq on a rediscovered hymn praising Babylon. It leans on quotes from the Assyriologist Enrique Jiménez. It prints a short excerpt in English and a photo caption for the tablet.

The Secret History of Knocking on Wood

Benjamin Breen. 24 February 2026. Personal newsletter essay, with data visualizations built by an AI tool.

The essay starts from a parent's observation of a toddler learning to knock on wood. It tests a 19th-century origin claim with a crowd-sourced list plotted on a globe. It then argues that unwritten gestures cannot be learned by AI from text.

Tablet of Destinies

Not stated (Wikipedia, edited by many contributors). Last edited 25 July 2026. Short encyclopedia article.

The article is brief and mostly summarises how the tablet appears in four ancient texts. It cites a few scholarly works and translations. It adds that the common name Tablets of Destiny is a mistranslation.

Charaktêres

Not stated (Wikipedia, written by volunteer editors). Last edited 1 August 2026. Encyclopedia stub article.

This is a Wikipedia stub of a few paragraphs. It defines charaktêres and reports one thesis that counted them. It gives their forms and where they appear, citing five scholarly works.

Bluesky post on the machine-checked logic of Nagarjuna, with replies

Posted by hotrollhottakes.bsky.social, with replies from many commenters. 3 August 2026. Bluesky thread linking a Substack essay and a Lean proof.

This is a Bluesky thread, not the essay it points to. The opening post claims a machine-checked result about Nagarjuna's logic. The poster answers questions in the replies. Commenters raise parallels, a validity objection and a consistency objection.

Assessors of Maat

Not stated (Wikipedia, written by volunteer editors). Last edited 13 August 2026. Encyclopedia article, with a table of the 42 judges.

The article describes the 42 Assessors of Maat and the Weighing of the Heart. Its main feature is a table of all 42 judges. The table compares Wilkinson's list of sins with the Papyrus of Ani's declarations. Evidence is citations to Egyptology books.

John Murray Spear

Not stated (Wikipedia contributors). Last edited 27 August 2026. Short encyclopedia article, collaboratively written.

The article sketches the life of a Universalist minister who became a spiritualist. It centers on the machine called the New Motive Power. It is short and cites mostly a biography and archive documents.

The two Christian saints who are secretly the Buddha

Signore Galilei. 30 August 2026. Short personal blog post, with footnoted sources.

The post retells the story of Barlaam and Josaphat. It traces the path from Sanskrit to Georgian. It then offers a brief reflection on religions borrowing from one another. It lists six footnotes but argues mainly by narrative.

Sol Ascendans - The Website of Alex Sumner

Alex Sumner. Newest post 29 September 2026. Personal blog front page, with reviews, essays and rituals.

The front page shows recent posts in the author's voice. They mix book and gig reviews, an obituary, a scrying exercise and a ritual text. The author writes as a practising member of Golden Dawn and masonic groups. The evidence is personal experience and opinion.

Upaya

Not stated (Wikipedia contributors). Last edited 1 November 2026. Encyclopedia article, collaboratively written.

The article defines upaya and sets out how Buddhist traditions use it. It gives two teaching images, the burning house and the empty fist, with long quotations. It ends with a short note on mindfulness programs.

Picts

Not stated. Last edited 3 November 2026. Wikipedia encyclopedia article.

The article gathers what is known of the Picts. Sources include Roman writers, Bede, Irish annals, stones and metalwork. It stresses how much of Pictish history comes from outsiders. It reports scholarly disputes about the name, origins, kingship and language. It notes farming communities and no known towns before the 12th century.

Descent of Inanna into the Underworld

Not stated (Wikipedia contributors). Last edited 4 November 2026. Encyclopedia article, collaboratively written.

The article covers the Sumerian and Akkadian versions, the cast and the plot. It reviews how scholars reconstructed the text from tablets. It then surveys competing interpretations, later echoes and modern readings.

Foucault's Pendulum

Not stated (Wikipedia, written by volunteer editors). Last edited 7 November 2026. Encyclopedia article on a novel.

The article covers Eco's novel with a plot summary, themes and comparisons. It reads the book as satire of conspiracy thinking. Evidence is citations to critics and interviews with Eco.

Sarah

Not stated. Undated. Encyclopedia article (Wikipedia), many contributors.

This is a reference article, not an argument. It sets out the Genesis story, then later Jewish, Christian and Islamic portrayals of Sarah. It also reports the scholarly debate over whether the patriarchs were historical.
