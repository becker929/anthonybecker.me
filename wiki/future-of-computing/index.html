Future of computing

22 sources imagining other ways computing could work.

Written by Claude Opus 5.59 November 2026, 17:53 UTC (09:53 Pacific)

These sources imagine other ways computers and networks could work. Several concern small, local or open social software. This page lists 22 sources, oldest first where dated.

How to run a small social network site for your friends

Darius Kazemi. 8 July 2019 (updated 12 November 2024). Long practical guide, drawn from one community's experience.

The guide draws on the author's experience running Friend Camp for about 50 people. It argues the work is social first and technical second. The evidence is the author's own site and a few larger servers that shut down. The author says the size figures lack studies behind them.

Building community out of strangers

Tracy Durnell. 30 November 2023. Personal blog post, with quotes from other bloggers.

The post is one blogger's case for the blogroll. It is part of a series on connection in the indie web. It rests on the author's own habits and on quotes from other bloggers. It has no data. The page also carries 52 replies and links from other sites.

Malleable software: Restoring user agency in a world of locked-down apps

Geoffrey Litt, Josh Horowitz, Peter van Hardenberg and Todd Matthews (Ink & Switch). June 2025. Research lab essay, with descriptions of the lab's own prototypes.

The essay is a vision statement from a research lab. It surveys older work on end-user customization and names three design patterns. It then describes the lab's prototypes, which it says it used in its own work. It reports no user studies.

Code⇄GUI bidirectional editing via LSP

James Vaughan. 1 July 2025. Personal blog post, with a small proof-of-concept demo.

The post describes a proof of concept built in hours. Its evidence is a short demo and the author's own survey of code-based CAD tools. The author does not plan to develop the demo further.

you are in a box

jyn. 14 July 2025. Personal blog essay, argument with examples.

This is part 1 of a series called the computer of the next 200 years. It surveys ways tools interoperate: compatibility, standards, FFI, shell pipes and RPC. The evidence is one shell pipeline and named tools, not measurements.

Cyborgs vs rooms, two visions for the future of computing

Matt Webb. 13 October 2025. Short personal blog post, argument only.

The post is a brief sketch, not a study. It lists examples of each vision and says which one the author prefers. It offers no data, only named projects and a stated preference.

the terminal of the future

jyn. 11 November 2025. Personal blog post, a design proposal with no built system.

This is part 6 of a series called the computer of the next 200 years. The post is a design proposal with a staged build plan. It cites Warp, iTerm2, Pluto.jl and tmux as partial precedents. It builds no prototype.

Atmospheric Computing

Paul Frazee. 31 December 2025. Personal blog post, advocacy with a short technical section.

The post names an idea, atmospheric computing, and ties it to the AT Protocol. It gives a few working apps as examples. It also has a short technical section that the author says non-engineers can skip.

How close are we to a vision for 2010?

Posted by ColinWright, with replies from many commenters. 23 February 2026. Hacker News discussion thread.

The thread discusses a blog post at shkspr.mobi that grades an old vision of 2010. Commenters mix praise with tangents on agents, privacy, immigration and phones. Evidence is anecdote and outside links, and the thread has 71 points.

Why I'm betting on ATProto (and why you should, too)

Brittany Ellich. 30 March 2026. Personal blog post after a conference.

A personal essay written after a conference. It blames ad-funded social media for division and loneliness. It presents ATProto as a way to own your data and build human-centered apps. The evidence is the author's own experience.

I'm building a parallel internet, and it's called The Thinnernet

Giovanni. 8 June 2026. Personal blog post, a speculative essay.

A speculative essay by a blogger who writes with a Steve Jobs alter-ego. It proposes a low-data tier of the internet so all users get a consistent experience. The evidence is analogy and argument, with no prototype.

SwiftUI After 7 Years: A Story of Mediocrity: Will it ever stop feeling like a beta?

Yakov Manshin. 20 July 2026. Opinion video essay with a written transcript.

A video essay by an Apple-platform developer, published with a full transcript. It argues SwiftUI has not become a production-grade framework. The evidence is personal experience, Apple's own tutorial project and one informal performance comparison.

Modern email can be built from borrowed parts

Andros Fenollosa. 27 July 2026. Personal blog post proposing a design, with a prototype.

A blog post sketching a successor to email, named HMTP, assembled from existing web standards. It walks through discovery, identity, delivery, encryption and spam. It links a single-file Python prototype.

Stop Making TUIs

Thomas & Erin Ptacek, per the site's copyright line; no byline. 20 August 2026. Personal blog post, with a tour of AI-generated apps.

The post tours native Mac apps that the author had an AI agent write. It then argues against TUIs and answers likely objections. The evidence is the author's own use of the apps, not tests. The author says the apps are for personal use.

Reintroducing Spaces

Daniel Holmgren. 27 August 2026. Protocol design essay on a personal blog.

A design essay by a designer of atproto Spaces. It states four design goals and compares spaces with the circles model. The evidence is argument and one example app, not tests.

“We want it to really confuse people, but also really make people happy.”

Marcin Wichary (Unsung blog). 28 August 2026. Personal blog essay with a short tour of examples.

The essay answers a John Gruber link to ArtfulType, a Markdown editor for old Macs. It offers three more projects as examples of deliberate time mixing. The evidence is the author's taste, plus quotes from a long read and an article.

We have whittled down all the nominations to our shortlist for the 2026 Tiny Awards!

Matt Muir. 1 September 2026. Bluesky post announcing an award shortlist.

A very short social post. It announces 11 shortlisted websites and links the voting page. The post does not name the entries.

A Love Letter to the Super Dimension Fortress

Brennan Kenneth Brown. 24 September 2026. Personal blog essay with two how-to sections.

A personal essay by a new member of SDF. It covers the system's history, its services and how to join. It ends with two hands-on sections, a Perl guestbook and notes on Dillo-friendly sites.

What Even Is An OS Now?

Thomas & Erin Ptacek, per the site's copyright line; no byline. 25 September 2026. Personal blog post, a prediction with a project announcement.

The author says they are leaving Fly.io for a new project and talking their book. The post is prediction and personal story. It offers no tests or data, and announces that the project is a phone.

TownSquare, a tiny presence layer for websites

Cauê Napier. Undated. Product landing page for a free website script.

The page is a short landing page with a live demo. It describes one script tag that adds a shared space to a site. The page is short and gives no technical detail.

bitchat

Not stated. Undated. Open-source project landing page.

A brief landing page in plain text with ASCII art. It states what bitchat is and links the iOS, macOS and Android source code. The page makes claims only and shows no tests.

Whyrusleeping, Protocol Labs Better Directions for Using Information

Whyrusleeping of Protocol Labs, published by Foresight Institute. Undated. Conference talk video; only the description was read.

The video is a talk by Whyrusleeping of Protocol Labs. Its description says it was recorded at a Foresight workshop in San Francisco. The video was not viewed and no transcript was available, so its content is unknown.

Glossary

ABI
The low-level rules for how compiled programs call each other.
ACL
Access control list: a list saying who may see or change something.
Agent
An AI model that can run tools and write code on its own.
Anachronism
Something placed in the wrong time period on purpose or by mistake.
ARPA membership
SDF's paid lifetime tier with extra tools and voting rights.
AT Protocol
An open standard for social apps where users keep their own accounts and data.
Atmosphere
The name for the network of apps built on ATProto.
ATProto
An open protocol for social apps where users keep their data.
Automerge
A library, made by the lab, that syncs shared documents between users.
BBS
A computer that people dialed into by modem to chat and share files.
Blogroll
A list of other blogs that a site's owner recommends or follows.
Bluetooth mesh
A network where nearby devices pass messages along to one another over Bluetooth.
CAD
Computer-aided design software for modelling parts and buildings.
Catalyst
Apple's tool for running iPad apps on the Mac.
CGI
An old method for running a script each time a web page is requested.
CLI
Command-line interface, where you type commands and read text output.
Code of conduct
A written set of rules for acceptable behavior on a site.
Compound document
A document built from parts that are edited by different programs.
Curses
An old library for drawing text interfaces in a terminal.
Cyborg
A person extended by wearable or implanted devices.
Decentralized protocol
A shared standard where no single company controls all the data.
Declarative UI
Describing the interface you want and letting the framework work out the steps.
Dennard scaling
The trend of chips getting faster and cooler as transistors shrank.
Dillo
A very small web browser with no JavaScript support.
DKIM
A signature scheme email servers use to check who sent a message.
Dynamicland
A project to make a room-sized computer people use together.
Emulator
Software that imitates another computer so old programs can run.
Eventual consistency
A design where copies of data may differ briefly and then agree.
Fantasy console
An imagined game machine that exists only as software.
Federation
Separate servers exchanging posts so users on different sites can follow each other.
FFI
Foreign function interface, a way for code in one language to call another.
Fixed-function application
A program built by others to do one set of defined tasks.
Foresight Institute
A nonprofit that supports technologies for the long-term future.
Fork
A modified copy of open source software kept as its own project.
Frontier model
One of the most capable AI models available.
GeometryReader
A SwiftUI view that reports its size so code can place things by hand.
Gopher and Gemini
Two lightweight text-focused internet protocols older or simpler than the modern web.
Hometown
A Mastodon fork by the author that adds local-only posting.
Hop
One relay step when a message passes through an intermediate device.
HPKE
A standard way to encrypt a message to someone using their public key.
HyperCard
A 1980s Mac program for building stacks of linked cards.
IETF
The standards body that publishes many internet protocols.
Immigration
The border check for people entering a country.
IndieWeb
A movement for people to publish on sites they own and link them together.
IPC
Inter-process communication, ways for separate programs to exchange data.
JMAP
A standard for reading and syncing mailboxes over JSON and HTTP.
Jupyter
A notebook tool where code runs in cells and output appears beneath each cell.
Latency
The delay between a request and its response.
Local-first software
Software that keeps data on the user's device and still lets people collaborate.
LSP
Language Server Protocol, a standard way for editors to get code features from a server.
Malleable software
Software that users can change easily to fit their own needs.
Markdown editor
A writing app that uses plain-text marks for headings and emphasis.
Mastodon
Free software for running a Twitter-like social network on your own server.
MeshCore
A radio mesh network for sending text messages without cellular service.
Modality
The kind of content an app deals with, such as video or recipes.
Modding
Changing software without the original developer's permission.
MQTT
A lightweight messaging protocol with small headers.
MX record
A DNS entry saying which server receives mail for a domain.
OAuth consent screen
The screen that asks what an app may do with an account.
OpenSCAD
A free CAD program in which models are written as code.
Operating system
The core software that manages hardware and keeps apps separate.
Orthogonal persistence
Keeping program state saved automatically, apart from the program's own logic.
P-Comm
The personal communicator device imagined in the 2010 vision.
Parasocial
A one-sided bond with someone who does not know the reader.
PDS
Personal Data Server: the server that stores a user's account and data.
Peer-to-peer
A design where devices talk directly rather than through a central server.
Personal data store
A server that holds one user's records and signs them.
Plugin
An add-on that extends an app in ways its developer allows.
Presence layer
A feature that shows who else is on a page right now.
Protocol Labs
A company behind decentralized data and networking projects.
Provenance
Where a piece of software came from and who made it.
PTY
A pseudo-terminal, a kernel link between a terminal window and the programs in it.
QoS
Quality of service: rules that give some network traffic priority over other traffic.
RPC
Remote procedure call, a defined interface for one program to call another.
RSS feed reader
An app that collects new posts from many sites in one place.
Script tag
A line of HTML that loads a program into a web page.
SDF
A nonprofit public-access UNIX system and online community started in 1987.
Shell integration
Extra signals that tell a terminal where commands begin and end.
Shortlist
A reduced list of nominees from which a winner is chosen.
Sigchain
A chain of keys where each new key is signed by the previous one.
SMTP
The old protocol that servers use to send email to each other.
Somaforming
Changing ourselves to fit an environment, as opposed to terraforming.
Space
A group of records with one owner and one shared audience.
SwiftUI
Apple's framework for building app interfaces by declaring what they should look like.
Switching cost
The effort needed to move from one tool to another.
Thinnernet
The author's name for a low-data lane of the internet.
Tiny Awards
An awards scheme for websites, with a 2026 round and public voting.
Tmux
A tool that keeps terminal sessions running so you can detach and reattach.
TUI
Terminal user interface, a program with windows and menus drawn in text characters.
Tweetcart
A tiny PICO-8 game whose code fits in one tweet.
Ubiquitous computing
Computing embedded in everyday objects and surroundings.
UIKit and AppKit
Apple's older interface frameworks for iOS and macOS.
WebFinger
A standard for looking up information about an account from its address.
Webmention
A standard way for one site to tell another that it linked to it.
WebSocket
A web connection where server and page can send messages both ways.
XMPP
An open chat standard that many companies once used to link their services.
