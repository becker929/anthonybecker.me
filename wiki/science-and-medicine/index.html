Science and medicine

Fourteen sources from research papers and science writing.

Written by Claude Opus 5.59 November 2026, 17:53 UTC (09:53 Pacific)

These sources are a mixed set of research papers and science writing. Pages describe what each reports, and none of them is medical advice. This page lists fourteen sources, oldest first where dated.

Themes here: What lasts (2), Saying it exactly (1).

Spontaneous Emergence of Hierarchy in Biological Systems

Jiankui He, Rice University. November 2010. Doctoral thesis in bioengineering, mostly computational analysis.

The thesis collects six research chapters under advisor Michael Deem. Topics range from protein networks to world trade, influenza, CRISPR and antibody genes. The evidence is computational analysis of sequence, network and trade data.

Psychological impact of polygamous marriage on women and children: a systematic review and meta-analysis

Ismail Shaiful Bahari and four co-authors. 13 December 2021. Peer-reviewed systematic review and meta-analysis (BMC Pregnancy and Childbirth).

The review pools 23 cross-sectional studies from a few countries. It reports higher depression among women and higher symptom scores among children in polygamous marriages. The authors say causation cannot be shown and most studies come from Arab societies.

Ten Basic Clouds

National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA), JetStream educational site. Last updated 28 March 2023. Government educational reference page.

This is an educational page from NOAA's JetStream site. It lists ten cloud types in three height bands, with a short definition of each. The definitions come from the World Meteorological Organization's cloud atlas. The page is reference text with no experiments.

A primer on molecular dynamics

Abhishaike Mahajan, Owl Posting. May 20, 2024. Explainer blog post on Substack, with two paper walk-throughs.

The post is a self-taught explainer written after about a month of study. It walks through system setup, force fields, equilibration, production runs and free energy. It then reads two published papers using these ideas.

Adipose tissue retains an epigenetic memory of obesity after weight loss

Laura C. Hinte, Daniel Castellano-Castillo and colleagues, ending with Ferdinand von Meyenn, ETH Zurich. 18 November 2024. Peer-reviewed research article in Nature, with human biopsies and mouse experiments.

The paper combines human fat biopsies with a detailed mouse study of fat cells. It reports lasting changes in gene activity and chromatin marks after weight loss. Mice carrying these changes regained weight faster on a high-fat diet. The authors state that causation is not proven.

First baby born in UK to woman with transplanted womb

Posted by gmays, with replies from many commenters. 17 April 2025. Hacker News discussion thread on a news report.

The thread has 300 points and comments from many users. Much of it drifts from the news into debates on gender, cloning and evolution. It offers opinion, anecdote and a few links, not new evidence.

A generic non-invasive neuromotor interface for human-computer interaction

Patrick Kaifosh, Thomas R. Reardon and CTRL-labs at Reality Labs. 23 July 2025. Peer-reviewed open-access journal paper, with tests on new users.

The paper comes from CTRL-labs at Reality Labs. It reports a wristband, a large training set and tests with new users. Evidence includes offline accuracy, closed-loop speeds and comparisons with a trackpad and a game controller.

Robert Hooke's "Cyberpunk" letter to Gottfried Leibniz

mynamelowercase, on the blog Drawn In Perspective. 1 November 2025. Personal blog post about an archival letter, with reader comments.

The post presents a partly transcribed letter from the Royal Society archives. The author argues it shows an early hacker mindset. Evidence is one letter, a few quotes, and the author's own reading.

Brain has five 'eras' with adult mode not starting until early 30s

Posted by hackernj, with replies from many commenters. 25 November 2025. Hacker News discussion thread on a news article.

The thread has 324 points and comments from many users. It mixes personal stories, doubts about the study and side debates on parenting and law. It offers anecdote and argument, not new data.

FDA approves first gene therapy for treatment of genetic hearing loss

Posted by JeanKage, with replies from many commenters. 27 April 2026. Hacker News discussion thread about an FDA press release.

The source is a long thread, not the press release. It mixes medical caveats, personal stories and heated exchanges. Topics include federal policy and Deaf culture. The FDA release supplies the trial figures.

Atomically precise mechanosynthesis of carbon structures on hydrogenated Si(100) by inverted-mode STM

Megan Cowie and many co-authors, CBN Nano Technologies, Inc., Ottawa. 26 May 2026. Experimental physics preprint (arXiv version 1) with simulations.

The paper describes placing C2 units on hydrogen-covered silicon at 4 K. Evidence is microscope images matched to simulations, plus outcome counts from hundreds of attempts. The authors work for the company that funded the study.

How Many Elementary Particles Are There, Really?

Natalie Wolchover, columnist, Quanta Magazine. 15 June 2026. Magazine column (Qualia essay) built on interviews with physicists.

The essay tallies particles under different counting rules, from 17 up to 995.5. It draws on interviews with four physicists and one 2011 theorem. It describes the counts but does not prove them.

We accidentally built a synthetic cell factory

Akshay Maheshwari, CEO, b.next. Undated. Company blog post about its own manufacturing work.

The CEO of b.next tells how an open toolkit became a factory. The post is a company narrative with photos and a few counts. It offers no performance data for the kits.

Animals could easily be talking to us if we tried

Evan's Weekly Newsletter (full name not given in the text). Undated. Short newsletter post, speculative proposal.

The post is two short paragraphs. It proposes a wearable rig that sends a dog's brain data to an AI. The AI would predict speech. It offers argument only, with no test or prototype.

Glossary

AAV vector
A harmless virus used to carry a gene into cells.
Adipose tissue
Body fat, made mostly of fat-storing cells called adipocytes.
Antiparticle
A twin of a particle with opposite electric charge.
ATAC-seq
A method that finds regions of DNA that are open and accessible.
Atomically precise fabrication
Building structures with every atom placed at a chosen position.
Bariatric surgery
Stomach surgery used to cause large weight loss.
Boson
A particle that carries a force, or the Higgs particle.
Brain eras
The study's name for periods of brain organization that change at certain ages.
Brain-computer interface
A system that turns nerve or brain signals into computer commands.
Character error rate
The share of typed characters that differ from the intended text.
Characteristica Universalis
Leibniz's planned universal symbolic language for expressing and mechanising reasoning.
Chirality
A left or right handedness of matter particles, which are mirror images.
Closed-loop
Tested live, with the user seeing the result of the decoder in real time.
Codependency
A pattern of imbalanced relationships where one person enables another's harmful behavior.
Compositional age
A thesis method that dates a protein by how many old amino acids it contains.
Confidence interval
A range that shows the uncertainty around an estimate.
Corona
A coloured ring close around the sun or moon.
CRISPR
A bacterial immune system that stores snippets of virus DNA as spacers.
Cross-sectional study
A study that measures people at a single point in time.
Cybernetics
The study of control and communication in machines and living things, named by Norbert Wiener.
Cyberpunk
A science fiction genre about high technology, urban sprawl and do-it-yourself counterculture.
Cytosol
The post's name for PURE-like defined materials sold as kits for building synthetic cells.
Dangling bond
An unpaired bond on a surface atom that can react.
Deaf culture
The shared language and community of many people who are Deaf, centered on sign language.
Degrees of freedom
The independent ways a field or particle can vary.
Enhanced sampling
Tricks that change the simulation rules to explore molecular states faster.
Enhancer
A DNA stretch that boosts the activity of a gene.
Epigenetic
Chemical marks on DNA or its packaging that alter gene activity, not the sequence.
Equilibration
A warm-up stage that brings the system to the target temperature and pressure.
Femtosecond
One millionth of a billionth of a second, the usual simulation time step.
Fermion
A matter particle, such as an electron or a quark.
Force field
A set of equations and constants that give the energy of a molecular system.
Functional ultrasound
A non-invasive imaging method that tracks changes in blood flow in the brain.
Gene regulatory network
The web of genes that switch each other on and off during development.
Gene therapy
A treatment that gives cells a working copy of a faulty gene.
Gluon
The particle that carries the strong force between quarks.
Halo
A ring of light seen around the sun or moon.
Histone mark
A chemical tag on DNA-packaging proteins that can turn genes up or down.
Hydrogen-passivated
Covered with hydrogen atoms so the surface is unreactive.
Immunosuppressants
Drugs that weaken the immune system so it does not reject a transplant.
IVF
In vitro fertilization, where an egg is fertilized outside the body.
Longitudinal study
A study that follows the same people over time.
Mechanosynthesis
Building chemical bonds by positioning molecules with mechanical motion, not heat or light.
Meta-analysis
A statistical pooling of results from several studies.
Microchimerism
A few cells from another person, such as a fetus, living in the body.
Modularity
The degree to which a network splits into groups that link mostly inside themselves.
Molecular dynamics
A computer method that simulates how atoms move over time under physical forces.
Motor unit action potential
The electrical pulse from one nerve cell and the muscle fibres it drives.
MRKH syndrome
A rare condition where the womb is missing or underdeveloped from birth.
Odds ratio
A measure comparing how likely an outcome is in one group against another.
OTOF
A gene needed for otoferlin, a protein that passes sound signals in the ear.
Plasmid
A small DNA loop used to carry a gene into a host.
Polygamy
Marriage involving more than two spouses, mostly one husband with several wives in these studies.
Prefrontal cortex
The front part of the brain linked to planning and self-control.
Promoter
A DNA stretch near a gene where its reading begins.
PURE system
A defined mix of purified proteins, tRNA and ribosomes that makes proteins in a tube.
QM/MM
A simulation method combining quantum mechanics near the reaction with simpler mechanics elsewhere.
Quantum field theory
A mathematical framework treating particles as ripples in fields that fill space.
Ribosome
The molecular machine that builds proteins from genetic instructions.
Royal Society
A learned society of scientists founded in England in the 17th century.
Scanning tunnelling microscope (STM)
An instrument that images surfaces atom by atom with a current through a sharp tip.
SCL-90
A 90-item questionnaire that scores a range of psychological symptoms.
SEMG
Surface electromyography, which records muscle electrical signals through the skin.
Si(100)
A common flat face of a silicon crystal.
Single-arm trial
A study with no control group, compared against known outcomes.
Single-nucleus RNA sequencing
A method that reads which genes are active in each cell nucleus.
Standard Model
The set of equations describing all known elementary particles and their interactions except gravity.
Synthetic cell
A cell-like system built from defined parts rather than grown from a living cell.
Systematic review
A review that searches for and screens studies by set rules.
TRNA
Short RNA molecules that bring amino acids to the ribosome during protein making.
VDJ recombination
The shuffling of gene segments that creates varied antibodies.
Virga
Streaks of rain that evaporate before reaching the ground.
Voice AI
Software that turns data or text into spoken audio.
WMO
The World Meteorological Organization, which sets worldwide standards for weather observation.
Womb transplant
A surgery that places a donor uterus in a recipient to carry a pregnancy.
